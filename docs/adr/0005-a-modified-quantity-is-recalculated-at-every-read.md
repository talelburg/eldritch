# A modified quantity is recalculated at every read

The Rules Reference defines every modified quantity as a live recalculation, not a result you compute once. `glossary/Modifiers.md`:

> The game state constantly checks and (if necessary) updates the count of any variable value or quantity that is being modified.
>
> Any time a new modifier is applied (or removed), the entire quantity is recalculated from the start, considering the unmodified base value and all active modifiers.

The engine computed several such quantities once and stored the result. A skill test's difficulty was snapshotted into `InFlightSkillTest.difficulty` at initiation and never re-read; the total skill value was summed at ST.3, *before* the ST.4 chaos-symbol effects it can depend on; a `ModifierScope::ThisSkillTest` modifier was pushed onto a flat `Vec` keyed only by investigator, so it could be created outside any test and spend itself on a test rounds later; and the whole modifier vocabulary addressed exactly one entity, the acting investigator. We decided that **every modified quantity in the engine is a query over live state, evaluated at the moment it is read**, and that the query is a single five-stage fold in RR's own order — base, row transform, additive, multiplicative, substitution — fed by two populations: modifiers *derived* from where cards sit, and modifiers *recorded* for lifetimes that outlive any card's zone.

Beat Cop 01018 is why the read time matters rather than merely the arithmetic. `Appendix_II_Timing_and_Gameplay.md` orders ST.4 ("Apply chaos symbol effect(s)") before ST.5, and ST.5 reads *"all active card abilities that are modifying the investigator's skill value"* — active **at ST.5**. Beat Cop reads *"You get +1 [combat]."* with printed health 2. In The Gathering, a `[tablet]` deals 1 damage when a Ghoul is present; soak that onto an already-damaged Beat Cop at ST.4 and the ally is discarded, so its `+1 [combat]` must be gone by ST.5. The engine summed at ST.3 and a test that should have failed by 1 succeeded. No arithmetic fix reaches that — the sum has to happen later, and it has to re-derive rather than re-use.

## Population: derived where a card's zone is the truth, recorded where it isn't

Most modifiers are true exactly while a card sits somewhere, so the scan over those places *is* the population and there is no bookkeeping to get wrong. It also disposes of the conditional cases for free, and there are many of them: Screeching Byakhee 01175 (*"While engaged with an investigator with remaining sanity 4 or fewer, Screeching Byakhee gets +1 fight and +1 evade."*) and Avian Thrall 02094 (*"While Avian Thrall is being attacked using a [[Ranged]], [[Firearm]], or [[Spell]] asset, it gets -3 fight."*) have no "applied" or "removed" moment to hook — they blink on and off as sanity drops or a different weapon is chosen.

A minority have a lifetime decoupled from any zone, and those are recorded explicitly: `ThisSkillTest`, `ThisTurn`, until end of phase (Encyclopedia 01042 — *"[action] Exhaust Encyclopedia: Choose an investigator at your location. That investigator gets +2 to a skill of your choice until the end of the phase."*), the revealed chaos token's ±N, and the one-shot "+N for this attack" a weapon grants. There is nothing to scan for these; the asset stays in play and exhausted while the buff runs and expires independently of it.

Both populations feed one fold, and a recorded row stores its **expression**, not a resolved integer, evaluated at read time like the derived ones. Esoteric Formula 02254 forces this: *"You get +2 [willpower] for this attack for each clue on the attacked enemy."* Freezing that to a number at push time reintroduces the same class of bug one level down.

The scan sweeps every place a card that modifies something can sit — all investigators' in-play cards and investigator cards, all locations and their attachments, all enemies and their attachments, and the current act and agenda. Narrower is wrong, not merely incomplete: Whippoorwill 02090 is an *enemy* modifying every investigator at its location, Cold Spring Glen 02244 a *location* modifying enemies in it, and The Ritual Begins 01144 an *agenda* modifying every enemy (*"Each enemy gets +1 fight and +1 evade."*). None is reachable from a controller-keyed scan of one investigator's own cards.

## The fold

Five stages, each with a corpus consumer:

1. **Base.** The printed value, unless replaced. `glossary/Base_Value.md`: *"Base value is the value of an element before any modifiers are applied. **Unless otherwise specified**, the base value of an element derived from a card is the value printed on that card."* Duke 02014 (*"You attack with a base [combat] skill of 4."*) and Strange Solution 02263/02264 are the "otherwise specified" — and because they replace only the *base*, everything in stages 2–4 still stacks on top. The official FAQ says so of a base replacement directly, on George Barnaby 11017: *"While George Barnaby's ability sets his base maximum hand size to be equal to the number of facedown cards beneath him (rather than the default value of 8), **this value can then be modified by other abilities**."* (`data/official-faq/Frequently_Asked_Questions.md`).
2. **Row transform.** A modifier whose subject is another modifier. Jim Culver 02004 — *"Treat the modifier on [skull] tokens you reveal as '0.'"*, whose ruling is emphatic that the substitution is narrow: *"Only treat the **modifier** on [skull] skull tokens as 0, not the whole token. It's still a skull token, so any additional effects (from the scenario reference card, **Spells**, etc.) trigger."* (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/02004>) — and Hunting Nightgaunt 01172, *"While attempting to evade Hunting Nightgaunt, double the negative modifier of each revealed chaos token."*
3. **Additive and subtractive.**
4. **Doubling and halving**, then rounding. `Modifiers.md`: *"while performing the calculation, all additive and subtractive modifiers are calculated before doubling and/or halving modifiers"* and *"Fractional values are rounded up after all modifiers have been applied."* Double or Nothing 02026 doubles a difficulty; Hunting Nightgaunt doubles a row.
5. **Clamp, then substitution.** `Modifiers.md` puts the clamp last — *"after all active modifiers have been applied, any resultant value below zero is treated as zero"* — and its worked example makes the ordering load-bearing: Danny's agility 4 with a −8 token and a +2 from "Lucky!" is 4 − 8 + 2 = −2 → 0, **not** (4 − 8 → 0) + 2 = 2. Automatic failure and success then substitute the whole quantity: *"If a skill test automatically fails, the investigator's total skill value for that test is considered 0. If a skill test automatically succeeds, the total difficulty of that test is considered 0."* Automatic failure takes precedence over automatic success: *"If a skill test both automatically succeeds and automatically fails, the automatic failure takes precedence, and the test automatically fails."*

Stage 2 is a predicate over rows evaluated at read time, not an address. No card names an individual modifier — Jim Culver names a class (`[skull]` rows this investigator revealed), Hunting Nightgaunt names a class under a live condition, whose ruling confirms the liveness: *"The ability ceases to apply if you play Sure Gamble."* (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01172>)

Stage 5 is where the `[auto_fail]` chaos token lands, which collapses `TokenResolution`'s third variant into the same path as Rex Murphy 02002's elder sign (*"You may instead choose to automatically fail this skill test to draw 3 cards."*) and Stroke of Luck 02271. It does **not** carry the ST.3/ST.4 skip, which is a timing concern on a separate axis and belongs to #630: `glossary/Automatic_Failure_Success.md` is explicit that *"If a chaos token effect causes an investigator to automatically succeed or fail at a skill test, continue with Steps 3 and 4, as normal."* ADR 0007 has since answered #630: the substitution still happens at stage 5, but *whether* the test automatically fails or succeeds is one query resolved above the fold for the whole test, and the `InFlightSkillTest::token_resolution` field is retired rather than collapsed into stage 5.

## Identity and targets

A modifier scoped to a skill test carries that test's identity. `GameState` gains a monotonic `SkillTestId`, stamped onto `InFlightSkillTest` at `start_skill_test` and onto every test-scoped row; a row whose id does not match the in-flight test is inert. A `ThisSkillTest` modify resolved with no test in flight therefore has no id to stamp and rejects — which is what makes #571 §1's guard unnecessary rather than defensive. Hyperawareness 01034's *"[fast] Spend 1 resource: You get +1 [intellect] for this skill test."* is activatable at any player window and has no per-round limit (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01034>: *"You can use [fast] fast actions as many times as you want, as long as you can pay the cost; there is no limit."*), so nothing else bounds the leak. The id is also what Fire Axe 02032's *"(Limit three times per attack.)"* counts against.

The query is keyed on a `ModifierTarget` — investigator, location, enemy, or test — rather than a bare controller. `Enemy` is not forward compatibility: an enemy's fight and evade *are* the difficulty of a Fight or Evade test, so the same shipping cards that motivate the target also close the difficulty hat. Fourteen Core + Dunwich cards address a non-controller target; five modify another investigator's skills and nine an enemy's fight or evade.

Difficulty stops being a field. `InFlightSkillTest.difficulty`, `InFlightSkillTest.test_modifier`, `Effect::Fight`'s evaluated `combat_modifier`, and `Effect::Investigate`'s evaluated `shroud_modifier` all become rows over the appropriate target. The DSL fields survive — a card still declares "+N for this attack" — but the evaluator translates them into rows instead of into snapshots. Flashlight 01087's *"Your location gets -2 shroud for this investigation"* is then live alongside Obscuring Fog 01168's *"Attached location gets +2 shroud"*, and the clamp happens once at stage 5 rather than twice at two different times.

`ModifierScope` stays the card author's vocabulary in `card-dsl`; the engine records a `Lifetime` with the `SkillTestId` or the turn or phase boundary already resolved. The two types diverge the moment a scope needs an identity baked in, so they are separate from the start.

## Considered options

**A central table of registered modifiers**, pushed on enter-play and popped on leave-play, was the shape the RR sentence most literally suggests — "applied (or removed)" reads as a push and a pop. It was rejected because a large share of the corpus's modifiers are conditional on live state, so rows go stale between the push and the read; the table would have to re-evaluate every row's condition anyway, at which point it is a cache rather than a source of truth and has bought only bookkeeping. Removal can be made automatic by giving rows a "while the source is in play" condition, but the *push* still needs a hook at every zone-entry path — play, attach, spawn, reveal, act/agenda advance, put-into-play-from-discard, setup — and a missed push is a silently-absent modifier that the action log replays faithfully. A table also stores copies of ability-derived data, so a corrected card impl leaves any persisted `GameState` carrying stale rows.

**Keeping the sum at ST.3 and special-casing whether an ST.4 effect could have mattered** is what the code did, under an in-comment justification (*"The in-scope `immediate` effects (damage/horror) don't change the skill value"*) that the Beat Cop trace falsifies. Any such predicate has to be re-derived every time a new symbol effect lands.

**An additive-only fold, with substitution and multiplication deferred**, was proposed and withdrawn: both tiers have shipping consumers. Duke and Strange Solution need stage 1, Jim Culver and Hunting Nightgaunt stage 2, Double or Nothing and Hunting Nightgaunt stage 4. Deferring them would have shipped a layer that is wrong by construction for cards already in `PACK_FILES`.

**Collapsing base replacement and automatic failure into one "override" tier** was an early error worth recording, because the two look alike and sit at opposite ends: Duke replaces the value *before* modifiers, auto-fail replaces the total *after* them. A single tier gets one of them wrong whichever end it is placed at.

**Modelling automatic failure and success as two independent stage-5 substitutions with no precedence** is what the Rules Reference alone yields, and it produces a wrong answer: skill value 0 against difficulty 0 compares as a success, on a collision stage 5's two corpus cards make reachable. The official FAQ resolves it in favour of failure.

**Widening the scan per card as each one lands** was rejected as the route to six near-identical scan functions, which is the outcome #628 was filed to prevent.

## Consequences

`resolved` is set at `DetermineOutcome` rather than at `Resolving`, so the documented invariant on `InFlightSkillTest::resolved` — "the outcome is known iff the test is past the commit window" — shifts by one driver step, and the ST.5 sum now runs after any player input a soak window collected. That is the point, and it is also the risk: anything that reads a total between ST.3 and ST.6 reads it before it exists.

The query returns a breakdown — base plus attributed contributions — rather than a bare `i8`. This is what recovers the one real advantage a registered table had (it can answer "why is my combat 5?"), and it lets engine tests assert on the composition rather than only the total.

Reconstructing an `EvalContext` at read time is the substantive cost, and it lands in the first slice rather than the last. The queries are `&GameState` reads in places the evaluator's machinery is not currently reachable; if that turns out to be intractable the whole decision is in question, which is why it is sequenced first.

Stage 2 exists but stays narrow. A *filter* over rows — 83001's `[tablet]`, *"For this skill test, ignore all bonuses to your skill value"* — has no Chapter 1 corpus consumer and is not built; the stage's signature is a transform over the row set, so a filter slots in without reshaping the fold.

The precedence rule at stage 5 is **not in the rules reference**. `glossary/Automatic_Failure_Success.md`, `Appendix_II_Timing_and_Gameplay.md`, `glossary/Cannot.md`, `glossary/Priority_of_Simultaneous_Resolution.md`, and `glossary/Choices_and_the_Grim_Rule.md` were all checked and none states it; Stroke of Luck 02271's parenthetical (*"unless a [auto_fail] token was revealed"*) is that card's own text, not a general rule. It is answered in the official FAQ's Q&A, which is now vendored — `data/official-faq/Frequently_Asked_Questions.md`, quoted at stage 5 above. That vendoring was **#672**, filed because this was the one clause in this ADR with no local citation.

Duke 02014 and Strange Solution 02263/02264 motivate stage 1 but are not implemented by it — they are card work with their own tests, tracked on **#673**. The stage exists regardless, because a fold without a base-replacement hook is wrong for them by construction.
