# A triggering condition resolves inside its own sequence

The Rules Reference does not describe a triggering condition as a moment with abilities hanging off it. It describes a **sequence**, and puts the condition's own resolution *inside* that sequence — `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md`: *"Each time a triggering condition occurs, the following sequence is followed: 1) execute "when..." effects that interrupt that triggering condition, (2) resolve the triggering condition, and then, (3) execute "after..." effects in response to that triggering condition."* `glossary/At.md` (identical in `glossary/If.md`) adds the middle cell: *"Some abilities have triggering conditions that use the words "at" or "if" instead of specifying "when" or "after," such as "at the end of the round," or "if the Ghoul Priest is defeated." These abilities trigger in between any "when..." abilities and any "after..." abilities with the same triggering condition."*

The engine's timing coordinator walked the three cells but had nowhere to perform step 2: every emit site mutated the board first and *then* emitted, which is why an ability printed **"when"** could only ever resolve after the thing it says it interrupts. So the coordinator frame gains a fourth cursor position — `When → ResolveCondition → At → After` — and `Continuation::EmitEvent` carries it as `step`. The frame is serialized as part of game state, so the step cannot hold a closure: it is dispatched from the timing event's **own value**, through `TimingEvent::condition_resolution`.

Who performs step 2 is an **exhaustive classification**, `ConditionResolution`, with two arms. **Coordinator-owned** means the coordinator resolves the condition between the when and at cells, so the when cell is walked and an interrupt there resolves before the condition's impact lands — what the card prints. **Caller-owned** means the emitting call site still mutates and then emits, so by the time the coordinator runs the condition has already resolved. Exhaustiveness is the enforcement: a new timing event cannot compile without a decision, the discipline ADR 0004 established for classifying continuation frames.

A caller-owned condition's when cell is **not walked**, and an ability declaring interrupt timing on one is **rejected**, naming the condition and this ADR. Resolving that interrupt would resolve it after the thing it was meant to interrupt — a wrong answer dressed as a right one — and dropping it silently is the failure mode this project forbids everywhere else. The reject is what keeps "never silently no-op" true while the migration is in flight.

**The bare milestones are a class, not a list of exceptions.** A condition is a **bare milestone** when nothing about the game state changes as the condition itself resolves: there is no mutation for a caller to have performed ahead of the emit and none for the coordinator to perform, so its resolve step is a documented no-op and its when cell is genuinely safe to walk. The test is not "does nothing happen around it" — plenty happens — but *whose* the teardown is: on a bare milestone the teardown belongs to the frame that emitted the condition, and runs **after the whole sequence** on a resume, rather than between the when and at cells. Five conditions are members, and each earns it the same way:

- **`RoundEnded`**, born coordinator-owned. The round-end teardown — expiring "until the end of the round" effects, Upkeep → Mythos — runs on the Upkeep anchor's `AfterRoundEnd` resume, not as the condition's impact, which is what act 01109 The Barrier's *"**Objective** - When the round ends, investigators in the hallway may, as a group, spend the requisite number of clues to advance."* has been resolving in all along.
- **`GameEnd`**, migrated by #720 because Cover Up 01007 prints *"**Forced** - When the game ends, if there are any clues on Cover Up: You suffer 1 mental trauma."* The scenario's ending is *already* structurally after the whole sequence: the `ScenarioEnd` frame advances its own cursor to `Finalize` and emits in tail position, and the ending finishes at the apply boundary — the victory-display scan and the module's `apply_resolution` — once every ability the emit queued has drained. That is exactly why the trauma's interactive acknowledge can span `apply` calls today.
- **`EliminationGameEnd`**, migrated alongside it and for the same card. Rules Reference p.10 Elimination step 0 fires *"when the game ends"* abilities on an eliminated investigator's in-play weaknesses, so Cover Up's one declaration is scanned at both points — the ruling is explicit that it fires here: *"If Roland is eliminated (by being defeated or taking a **resign** action) while Cover Up is in play, Cover Up's **Forced** effect triggers"* (`data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01007.md`). Steps 1–6 run on the re-exposed `Elimination` frame at `EliminationStep::RunSteps`, after another tail-position emit — and so does the sentence that reads most like this condition's own impact. That sentence is step 0's own tail, *"Then, remove those weaknesses from the game."*, which the engine discharges through step 1's threat-area partition rather than at the emit site; it lands after the whole sequence either way. Migrating one of the pair without the other would have made the retag reject the very elimination it was meant to serve.
- **`PhaseStarted`**, born coordinator-owned with the phase-boundary wiring (#697). `Appendix_II_Timing_and_Gameplay.md` step 1.1 calls the beginning of a phase *"an important game milestone that may be referenced in card text"* and describes no mutation, because there is none: what a phase beginning *does* is entirely the driver's — the Mythos round bump and doom, the Enemy phase's hunter movement, each phase's first player window — and every one of those runs on the emitting anchor's own `AfterPhaseStartForced` resume, after the whole sequence. It is the first member born under `ConditionResolution::Caller`'s own rule that *"a new timing event has no legacy emit site to invert, so it is born `Coordinator`"*, and the rule is what decided it: there was nothing to argue about, because there was no prior emit site at all.
- **`DamageAssigned`**, born with the damage model (ADR 0009). An assignment is a proposal — the Rules Reference has the tokens sitting *"next to the cards that will be taking the damage/horror"* — so nothing is on any card when the condition resolves, and the placement that looks like its impact belongs to `DamagePlaced`, its own coordinator-owned sibling one cursor step later on the `DealDamage` frame. It is the one member with a *second* condition rather than a resume for its teardown, which is what the named window between the two rules steps requires.

Neither game-end member is an exception to the migration discipline: both had a caller-owned emit site to invert, and inverting it cost nothing precisely because the tail-position emit ADR 0003 already required had left no mutation ahead of the emit to move. What #720 did have to move was a **fork predicate**: `has_weakness_game_end_ability` asked the step-0 scan whether elimination needed its frame at all, and asked it about the `after` cell only. A per-cell scan answering a whole-sequence question drops a retagged ability without a reject to show for it — the elimination takes its inline path and steps 1–6 remove the weakness before anything looks again — which is the same shape as the three `test_support` helpers #723 had to fix, and the reason the predicate now scans every cell.

Every other condition starts caller-owned and flips one at a time, when a card demands its when cell.

**A caller-owned condition is not automatically a single-cell one.** #702 generalized the walk to every triggering condition, and the `at` and `after` cells work on all of them regardless of who owns the resolve step — caller-owned only costs the `when` cell. Two conditions nevertheless bypassed the coordinator entirely for a while, both belonging to the enemy-attack machinery: `EnemyAttacks` (Dodge 01023's cancel) and `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` (Guard Dog 01021's soak retaliate). They shared one obstruction, and it was not ownership: their emit sites read the continuation stack *synchronously* after emitting — `open_windows()` in the attack loop — which is the shape ADR 0003 forbids, and which a coordinator frame breaks, because the window that frame will open is not open yet when the caller looks. #704 cleared it, and with it the last two per-condition tables: the one naming the single cell a condition's window opened at, and the `when`-timing whitelist of condition/pattern pairs in `trigger_matches`. A pattern now pairs with its condition identically in all three cells; which cell an ability resolves in is the coordinator's business alone, and forced-before-reaction has exactly one mechanism (the `TimingPoint` cursor) rather than two.

**Clue discovery was the third, and #703 migrated it — the worked example the contract promised.** `DiscoverClues` (Cover Up 01007's replacement) shared the obstruction in its cheapest form: its emit site peeked at the top window's kind to decide whether to discover now or defer, and its mutation was *already* factored into `perform_discovery` so a resume frame could call it. So the migration deleted the peek rather than restructuring a loop — `discover_clue` now caps the count and emits in tail position, and `resolve_clue_discovery` moves the clues at the coordinator's resolve step. That is what a migration costs when the condition's mutation is already a named function.

The migration also fixed where the **cancellation signal** is read. A replacement effect must prevent the condition from resolving — `glossary/Instead.md`: *"A replacement effect is an effect that replaces the resolution of a triggering condition with an alternate means of resolution."* — so the check belongs between the `when` cell and step 2, not at the closing window, and it is the coordinator that performs it for every coordinator-owned condition.

**A prevented condition takes the rest of its sequence with it.** #703 shipped the opposite — the cancel skipped step 2 and the `at` and `after` cells still ran — because the engine's `pending_cancellation` is one bool with no vocabulary for cancel versus nature-changing versus partial replacement, and no Core or Dunwich card declares an `at` or `after` ability on a discovery to observe the difference. #714 corrected it before #704 could make it a live corpus bug. Dodge 01023's ruling (`data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01023.md`) is the citation: *"If the attacking enemy has a **Forced** ability that says "When attacks" or "After attacks", that ability does not trigger if an attack is Dodged."* Note its scope — **both** later cells, and the `when` one too, since Dodge is itself a `when`-cell ability. `glossary/After.md` reaches the same place from the other side (after is *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition **has fully resolved**"*, and a prevented condition never fully resolves), as does `glossary/Instead.md` for Cover Up specifically: a `would` replacement that *changes the nature* of a triggering condition means *"No further abilities referencing the original triggering condition may be used."*

So the coordinator, reading the signal at its resolve step, **abandons the frame** rather than advancing the cursor to `At`; and `reaction_windows::withdraw_suppressed_candidates` empties an open `when`-cell window of every candidate still pending, reporting each as `LapseReason::ConditionPrevented`. The two halves are one rule seen from the frame and from the window: the cursor cannot suppress an option the player is already being offered, and the window cannot skip a cell that has not opened yet. Both are scoped to **coordinator-owned** conditions, and deliberately: a caller-owned one has already mutated the board and reads the signal at its own emit site, so the enemy attack inherited this on migration rather than gaining half of it early. #704 also made the ruling's own shape — a **Forced** ability suppressed by a dodge — reachable through a card, by giving the attack a forced trigger point that scans the *attacking enemy's* own card and implementing Silver Twilight Acolyte 01102 (*"**Forced** - After Silver Twilight Acolyte attacks: Place 1 doom on the current agenda."*). Dodging it places no doom.

**The enemy attack was the second migration, and the dear one — #704.** Its mutation was not a named function but a loop: `drive_attack_loop` emitted the interrupt point, read `open_windows()`, and parked or dealt accordingly, all inline. So migrating it meant restructuring the loop before the classification could move. The loop now parks on its `Continuation::AttackLoop` frame around **every** attack — head attacker left at the front of the list — emits `EnemyAttacks` in tail position, and lets the `drive` loop walk the attack's own sequence above it; `resolve_enemy_attack` places the damage and horror at the resolve step; and when the coordinator pops, the re-exposed loop frame takes the head off and moves on. Three suspension-shaped special cases went with it: the `BeforeAttack` / `AfterSoak` stage pair, the window-close resume `resume_enemy_attack`, and the single-soak-window-per-attack guard — a second soak condition is now just a second nested sequence.

**The attacker's exhaust is not part of the attack's impact.** It is the one piece of the old inline loop that could *not* move to the resolve step, because `data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01023.md` says a prevented attack still causes it: *"If an attack was cancelled during the Enemy phase, the attacking enemy still exhausts."* A `when`-cell cancel abandons the sequence before step 2, so an exhaust living there would be skipped. It belongs to the enemy phase's own step anyway — `data/rules-reference/rules/Appendix_II_Timing_and_Gameplay.md` on step 3.3: *"Upon completion of dealing the attack (and all abilities triggered by the attack), exhaust the enemy."* — which is *after* the `after` cell, not before it. So it moved onto the parked `AttackLoop` frame, where it runs once the whole sequence has popped. Two consequences worth stating: `EnemyExhausted` now follows a soak reaction rather than preceding it, and an attack that ends the scenario (the sole investigator's defeat) never exhausts its attacker at all, because the latched resolution cancels the parked loop along with everything else in the round (ADR 0004).

**The departure was the third migration — #721.** Barricade 01038 prints *"**Forced** - When an investigator leaves attached location: Discard Barricade."* (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/pack/core/core.json`), and `LeftLocation` was the inline kind of mutation #704 had made expensive: `move_primary_effect` dragged the engaged enemies to the destination (or disengaged those that could not follow), assigned the investigator's location, emitted `InvestigatorMoved`, revealed the destination, parked the entered-location half on its own frame, and only then emitted. So the prefactor came first, as its own commit and with the existing move coverage green across it: the departure block became `resolve_departure`, and only then did the arm flip. Make the change easy, then make the easy change.

**Two things deliberately did not move to the resolve step.** The entered-location half — auto-engagement and the `EnteredLocation` emit — stays on the `MoveEnter` frame parked beneath the emit, because that frame is #569's fix for entering resolving before leaving and this migration must not quietly undo it. And the destination *reveal* moved the other way, off `move_primary_effect` and **onto** that frame: it is the arrival's business, not the departure's, and after the migration the arrival happens further down the stack than the point the reveal used to sit at. The timing event gained a `destination` field to carry it, for the reason the ADR gives above — the resolve step is recomputed from the event's own value, so anything it needs has to be in the event rather than in the emitting call site's locals.

The retag it enables is invisible in the corpus: no Core or Dunwich card observes whether Barricade discards before or after the departure lands, so unlike #720 this fixed a wrong tag rather than a live bug. It was done anyway, because a permanently-waived row is the rot a self-clearing list exists to prevent, and because the terminal condition below — every arm flipped, the classification deleted — only stays reachable if the arms actually flip.

**One signal, two arms, and the third is #366.** The rules distinguish three ways a condition can be interfered with: a cancel (`glossary/Cancel.md`: *"Cancel abilities interrupt the initiation of an effect, and prevent the effect from initiating."*), a nature-changing `would` replacement, and a replacement that resolves the condition by *other means* without changing its nature. The first two suppress identically, so the engine spends no vocabulary telling them apart — the bool says *prevented*, and both set it. The third does not suppress, and it is **not modelled**: no corpus card wants one, and it is already recorded as **#366** (replace-with-different-impact), under the same norm that keeps DSL primitives out of the engine until two cards want the same pattern. A third modelled case waits on a card that contests it. The bool's other limit is unchanged: it is single-slot because Before-windows do not nest (TODO(#367)).

**The soak condition was the fourth migration, and it did not survive it — #727.** `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` walked the coordinator since #704 but stayed caller-owned, because the damage that triggered it had genuinely already been placed by the time it was emitted. The Rules Reference prints its worked example on Guard Dog 01021, whose reaction is a `when`, so #722 demanded that cell — and the demand could not be met by flipping an arm. The condition itself was wrong: one condition announced per damaged asset *after* a single collapsed call is neither of the two steps the rules deal damage in, which ADR 0009 quotes in full. So it was replaced by `DamageAssigned` (the bare milestone above) and `DamagePlaced` (coordinator-owned, resolving the simultaneous placement), sequenced by one `DealDamage` frame. **ADR 0009 is that decision**, and the reason a migration here cost a model rather than an arm.

## Considered options

**Inverting every emit site at once** — moving all fourteen caller-owned conditions' mutations behind the coordinator in one change — was rejected on the evidence of ADR 0003. That is the precise manoeuvre four call sites already got wrong: they read a queued emit's `Done` as "nothing happened" and ran their tails *above* the abilities they had just queued, which orphaned agenda 01107's forced end-of-enemy-phase Ghoul movement for a whole scenario (#569; ADR 0003 carries the trace and the printed text). Migrating one condition at a time costs a classification and a reject; migrating fourteen at once costs a bug class the engine has already paid for once.

**Letting an un-migrated condition's when cell resolve anyway** — collecting the interrupt and firing it after the caller's mutation — was rejected because it is the bug the whole spec exists to fix, only now with the engine's endorsement. An ability that resolves in the wrong cell is harder to notice than one that refuses.

**Storing the resolve step's action on the frame** was rejected on serialization grounds. Continuation frames round-trip through JSON; a fn pointer or closure does not. Recomputing the classification from the event's value at every visit costs one match and keeps the frame plain data.

**Making the classification a default-able trait or a lookup table** was rejected for the same reason ADR 0004 rejected it for continuation frames: a table is a thing a new variant can be forgotten from, and being forgotten is exactly the failure this classification exists to prevent.

## Consequences

**The reject is scaffolding with a terminal condition, and the terminal condition only holds if new events are born coordinator-owned.** When the last condition flips, the caller-owned arm has no members, the when cell is universally available, and the reject is unreachable; at that point the arm, the classification and the reject are deleted together. This is a **commitment**: caller-owned exists to migrate *existing* callers, not to accommodate new ones. A new timing event has no legacy emit site to invert, so there is no reason to reach for it, and reaching for it would make the scaffolding permanent.

**"At" and "if" resolve after the triggering condition's impact lands. This is an interpretation, not a citation.** No vendored source answers it — not the rules, not any per-card ruling, and not the official FAQ, which has now been vendored and checked. `glossary/When.md` pins when as *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition initiates, but before its impact upon the game state resolves"*; `glossary/After.md` pins after as *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition has fully resolved"*; `glossary/At.md` pins at only relative to those two.

The corpus decides it. A sweep of all 159 pack files in the Chapter 1 snapshot (recorded on #693) found **every "at" trigger is a bare milestone** — 269 instances across 18 clause forms, all phase, round, turn or step boundaries — so "at" is never contested by a condition's resolution at all. "If" is contested, in roughly 148 mutating instances dominated by `If <X> is defeated`, and the Core case settles it: **Ghoul Priest 01116 carries 2 victory points**, and act 01110 What Have You Done? prints *"**Objective** - If the Ghoul Priest is Defeated, advance."* with a scenario resolution on its back. If that objective fires when the defeat is *determined* rather than after it has *fully resolved*, the scenario ends with the Ghoul Priest never reaching the victory display, and two earned experience are lost with no recovery. The same shape recurs at The Experiment 02058 and at Silas Bishop 02216 behind act 02200. ADR 0004's Ghoul Priest trace already assumes this reading.

The gap survived the vendoring, which is worth recording, because #672 filed this as a worked example of what a local copy of the official FAQ would let us replace with a citation. It does not. The FAQ's own entry says only what `glossary/At.md` already said — *"(2.2) Timing of "At…" or "If…" abilities … These abilities trigger in between any "when…" abilities and any "after…" abilities with the same triggering condition"* (`data/official-faq/Rulings_and_Clarifications.md`) — and its sequence, *"(1) execute "when…" effects that interrupt that triggering condition, (2) resolve the triggering condition, and then, (3) execute "after…" effects"*, leaves "in between" straddling step 2 rather than landing on one side of it. So the interpretation stands on the corpus evidence above, and the citation gap is in the source rather than in our shelf.

**The gap survives a systematic re-read of the Q&A, not merely the vendoring.** All 145 pairs of the FAQ's *Frequently Asked Questions* section — the part that appears in no other vendored source — have been read against everything the engine implements, and the two candidate pairs were checked directly. **Pair 120** is about a Forced ability *nesting* and needing no player window, which `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md` and `glossary/Ability.md` already cite and which says nothing about cell ordering relative to a condition's impact; **pair 88** is about *attribution* — who counts as having put a location into play — not about trigger timing. `data/official-faq/Rulings_and_Clarifications.md:227` is verbatim identical to `glossary/At.md`: the same "in between", with no anchor to step 2. Settling this needs a new ruling from the publisher, not another pass over the shelf.

**The when cell has an unmodelled internal sub-order.** `glossary/Instead.md`: *"The word "would" is used to define the triggering condition of some abilities, and establishes a higher priority for those abilities than abilities referencing the same triggering condition without the word "would." (For instance, "When X would occur" resolves before "When X occurs.")"* The when cell therefore has two tiers inside it, and the engine implements one. No Core or Dunwich card contests it — nothing in the corpus puts a `would` ability and a bare `when` ability on the same condition — so this is recorded and not built. A card that contests it needs the sub-tier before it can be implemented correctly.

**The frame's shape is wire-visible, but it carries no compatibility shim, and that was checked rather than assumed.** `Continuation::EmitEvent` reaches every client as part of serialized game state, so the cursor field's rename from `bucket` to `step` looks like a break. It is not one: a coordinator frame exists only in memory, mid-sequence, between two `apply` calls, and the server persists a **seed state plus a `ResolveInput`-only action log** (`crates/server/src/session.rs`), replaying the log over the seed on load. The seed is post-seating and mulligan-pending, so its continuation stack cannot hold a coordinator frame, and no persisted artifact can carry the field. A serde alias for the old key was written and then removed: it would have been insurance against a persistence model this project does not have — per-action state snapshots — and a shim nothing can exercise is a claim of compatibility nobody has tested.
