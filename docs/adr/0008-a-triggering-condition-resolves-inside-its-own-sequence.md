# A triggering condition resolves inside its own sequence

The Rules Reference does not describe a triggering condition as a moment with abilities hanging off it. It describes a **sequence**, and puts the condition's own resolution *inside* that sequence — `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md`: *"Each time a triggering condition occurs, the following sequence is followed: 1) execute "when..." effects that interrupt that triggering condition, (2) resolve the triggering condition, and then, (3) execute "after..." effects in response to that triggering condition."* `glossary/At.md` (identical in `glossary/If.md`) adds the middle cell: *"Some abilities have triggering conditions that use the words "at" or "if" instead of specifying "when" or "after," such as "at the end of the round," or "if the Ghoul Priest is defeated." These abilities trigger in between any "when..." abilities and any "after..." abilities with the same triggering condition."*

The engine's timing coordinator walked the three cells but had nowhere to perform step 2: every emit site mutated the board first and *then* emitted, which is why an ability printed **"when"** could only ever resolve after the thing it says it interrupts. So the coordinator frame gains a fourth cursor position — `When → ResolveCondition → At → After` — and `Continuation::EmitEvent` carries it as `step`. The frame is serialized as part of game state, so the step cannot hold a closure: it is dispatched from the timing event's **own value**, through `TimingEvent::condition_resolution`.

Who performs step 2 is an **exhaustive classification**, `ConditionResolution`, with two arms. **Coordinator-owned** means the coordinator resolves the condition between the when and at cells, so the when cell is walked and an interrupt there resolves before the condition's impact lands — what the card prints. **Caller-owned** means the emitting call site still mutates and then emits, so by the time the coordinator runs the condition has already resolved. Exhaustiveness is the enforcement: a new timing event cannot compile without a decision, the discipline ADR 0004 established for classifying continuation frames.

A caller-owned condition's when cell is **not walked**, and an ability declaring interrupt timing on one is **rejected**, naming the condition and this ADR. Resolving that interrupt would resolve it after the thing it was meant to interrupt — a wrong answer dressed as a right one — and dropping it silently is the failure mode this project forbids everywhere else. The reject is what keeps "never silently no-op" true while the migration is in flight.

`RoundEnded` is the one condition born coordinator-owned, and it is worth being precise about why, because it looks like an exception and is not. The round ending is a **bare milestone**: nothing about the game state changes as the condition itself resolves. The round-end teardown — expiring "until the end of the round" effects, Upkeep → Mythos — runs *after* the whole sequence, on the Upkeep anchor's `AfterRoundEnd` resume, not as the condition's impact. There is no mutation for a caller to have performed ahead of the emit and none for the coordinator to perform, so its resolve step is a documented no-op and its when cell is genuinely safe to walk — which is what act 01109 The Barrier's *"**Objective** - When the round ends, investigators in the hallway may, as a group, spend the requisite number of clues to advance."* has been resolving in all along. Every other condition starts caller-owned and flips one at a time, when a card demands its when cell.

**A caller-owned condition is not automatically a single-cell one.** #702 generalized the walk to every triggering condition, and the `at` and `after` cells now work on all of them regardless of who owns the resolve step — caller-owned only costs the `when` cell. Two conditions still bypass the coordinator entirely, both belonging to the enemy-attack machinery: `EnemyAttacks` (Dodge 01023's cancel) and `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` (Guard Dog 01021's soak retaliate). They share one obstruction, and it is not ownership: their emit sites read the continuation stack *synchronously* after emitting — `open_windows()` in `combat::deal_head_and_maybe_park` — which is the shape ADR 0003 forbids, and which a coordinator frame breaks, because the window that frame will open is not open yet when the caller looks. Clearing it means the attack loop stops draining inline and parks on its `AttackLoop` frame for the `drive` loop to re-expose. The first is additionally interrupt-timed; both are #704, which owns that loop.

**Clue discovery was the third, and #703 migrated it — the worked example the contract promised.** `DiscoverClues` (Cover Up 01007's replacement) shared the obstruction in its cheapest form: its emit site peeked at the top window's kind to decide whether to discover now or defer, and its mutation was *already* factored into `perform_discovery` so a resume frame could call it. So the migration deleted the peek rather than restructuring a loop — `discover_clue` now caps the count and emits in tail position, and `resolve_clue_discovery` moves the clues at the coordinator's resolve step. That is what a migration costs when the condition's mutation is already a named function; #704's is dearer because its is not.

The migration also fixed where the **cancellation signal** is read. A replacement effect must prevent the condition from resolving — `glossary/Instead.md`: *"A replacement effect is an effect that replaces the resolution of a triggering condition with an alternate means of resolution."* — so the check belongs between the `when` cell and step 2, not at the closing window, and it is the coordinator that performs it for every coordinator-owned condition.

**A prevented condition takes the rest of its sequence with it.** #703 shipped the opposite — the cancel skipped step 2 and the `at` and `after` cells still ran — because the engine's `pending_cancellation` is one bool with no vocabulary for cancel versus nature-changing versus partial replacement, and no Core or Dunwich card declares an `at` or `after` ability on a discovery to observe the difference. #714 corrected it before #704 could make it a live corpus bug. Dodge 01023's ruling (`data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01023.md`) is the citation: *"If the attacking enemy has a **Forced** ability that says "When attacks" or "After attacks", that ability does not trigger if an attack is Dodged."* Note its scope — **both** later cells, and the `when` one too, since Dodge is itself a `when`-cell ability. `glossary/After.md` reaches the same place from the other side (after is *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition **has fully resolved**"*, and a prevented condition never fully resolves), as does `glossary/Instead.md` for Cover Up specifically: a `would` replacement that *changes the nature* of a triggering condition means *"No further abilities referencing the original triggering condition may be used."*

So the coordinator, reading the signal at its resolve step, **abandons the frame** rather than advancing the cursor to `At`; and `reaction_windows::withdraw_suppressed_candidates` empties an open `when`-cell window of every candidate still pending, reporting each as `LapseReason::ConditionPrevented`. The two halves are one rule seen from the frame and from the window: the cursor cannot suppress an option the player is already being offered, and the window cannot skip a cell that has not opened yet. Both are scoped to **coordinator-owned** conditions, and deliberately: a caller-owned one has already mutated the board and reads the signal at its own emit site, so the enemy attack inherits this when #704 migrates it rather than gaining half of it early. Which is also why the ruling's own shape — a **Forced** `when` ability — is proved at the unit seam and not through a card: no coordinator-owned condition with a `when`-cell cancel carries a forced trigger point yet, clue discovery least of all, since it has none.

**One signal, two arms, and the third is #366.** The rules distinguish three ways a condition can be interfered with: a cancel (`glossary/Cancel.md`: *"Cancel abilities interrupt the initiation of an effect, and prevent the effect from initiating."*), a nature-changing `would` replacement, and a replacement that resolves the condition by *other means* without changing its nature. The first two suppress identically, so the engine spends no vocabulary telling them apart — the bool says *prevented*, and both set it. The third does not suppress, and it is **not modelled**: no corpus card wants one, and it is already recorded as **#366** (replace-with-different-impact), under the same norm that keeps DSL primitives out of the engine until two cards want the same pattern. A third modelled case waits on a card that contests it. The bool's other limit is unchanged: it is single-slot because Before-windows do not nest (TODO(#367)).

The third is worth one more sentence, because its cheapness is an illusion of the engine's own making. Guard Dog is *modelled* `after`-timed, so nothing about which cell it resolves in is at stake today — but the card prints *"[reaction] **When** an enemy attack deals damage to Guard Dog: Deal 1 damage to the attacking enemy."* (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/pack/core/core.json`), and the Rules Reference prints its worked example on this exact card. The declaration is one of the mis-tagged six #694 retags, so the holdout is a `when` cell waiting to be demanded, not a condition that will never want one.

## Considered options

**Inverting every emit site at once** — moving all fourteen caller-owned conditions' mutations behind the coordinator in one change — was rejected on the evidence of ADR 0003. That is the precise manoeuvre four call sites already got wrong: they read a queued emit's `Done` as "nothing happened" and ran their tails *above* the abilities they had just queued, which orphaned agenda 01107's *"**Forced** - At the end of the enemy phase: Each unengaged [[Ghoul]] enemy moves 1 location towards the Parlor."* for a whole scenario (#569). Migrating one condition at a time costs a classification and a reject; migrating fourteen at once costs a bug class the engine has already paid for once.

**Letting an un-migrated condition's when cell resolve anyway** — collecting the interrupt and firing it after the caller's mutation — was rejected because it is the bug the whole spec exists to fix, only now with the engine's endorsement. An ability that resolves in the wrong cell is harder to notice than one that refuses.

**Storing the resolve step's action on the frame** was rejected on serialization grounds. Continuation frames round-trip through JSON; a fn pointer or closure does not. Recomputing the classification from the event's value at every visit costs one match and keeps the frame plain data.

**Making the classification a default-able trait or a lookup table** was rejected for the same reason ADR 0004 rejected it for continuation frames: a table is a thing a new variant can be forgotten from, and being forgotten is exactly the failure this classification exists to prevent.

## Consequences

**The reject is scaffolding with a terminal condition, and the terminal condition only holds if new events are born coordinator-owned.** When the last condition flips, the caller-owned arm has no members, the when cell is universally available, and the reject is unreachable; at that point the arm, the classification and the reject are deleted together. This is a **commitment**: caller-owned exists to migrate *existing* callers, not to accommodate new ones. A new timing event has no legacy emit site to invert, so there is no reason to reach for it, and reaching for it would make the scaffolding permanent.

**"At" and "if" resolve after the triggering condition's impact lands. This is an interpretation, not a citation.** The vendored rules do not answer it and neither does any ruling in the vendored FAQ corpus. `glossary/When.md` pins when as *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition initiates, but before its impact upon the game state resolves"*; `glossary/After.md` pins after as *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition has fully resolved"*; `glossary/At.md` pins at only relative to those two.

The corpus decides it. A sweep of all 159 pack files in the Chapter 1 snapshot (recorded on #693) found **every "at" trigger is a bare milestone** — 269 instances across 18 clause forms, all phase, round, turn or step boundaries — so "at" is never contested by a condition's resolution at all. "If" is contested, in roughly 148 mutating instances dominated by `If <X> is defeated`, and the Core case settles it: **Ghoul Priest 01116 carries 2 victory points**, and act 01110 What Have You Done? prints *"**Objective** - If the Ghoul Priest is Defeated, advance."* with a scenario resolution on its back. If that objective fires when the defeat is *determined* rather than after it has *fully resolved*, the scenario ends with the Ghoul Priest never reaching the victory display, and two earned experience are lost with no recovery. The same shape recurs at The Experiment 02058 and at Silas Bishop 02216 behind act 02200. ADR 0004's Ghoul Priest trace already assumes this reading.

The gap is recorded rather than papered over: it is noted on **#672**, the ticket for vendoring the official FAQ document, as a worked example of what that vendoring would let us replace with a citation.

**The when cell has an unmodelled internal sub-order.** `glossary/Instead.md`: *"The word "would" is used to define the triggering condition of some abilities, and establishes a higher priority for those abilities than abilities referencing the same triggering condition without the word "would." (For instance, "When X would occur" resolves before "When X occurs.")"* The when cell therefore has two tiers inside it, and the engine implements one. No Core or Dunwich card contests it — nothing in the corpus puts a `would` ability and a bare `when` ability on the same condition — so this is recorded and not built. A card that contests it needs the sub-tier before it can be implemented correctly.

**The frame's shape is wire-visible, but it carries no compatibility shim, and that was checked rather than assumed.** `Continuation::EmitEvent` reaches every client as part of serialized game state, so the cursor field's rename from `bucket` to `step` looks like a break. It is not one: a coordinator frame exists only in memory, mid-sequence, between two `apply` calls, and the server persists a **seed state plus a `ResolveInput`-only action log** (`crates/server/src/session.rs`), replaying the log over the seed on load. The seed is post-seating and mulligan-pending, so its continuation stack cannot hold a coordinator frame, and no persisted artifact can carry the field. A serde alias for the old key was written and then removed: it would have been insurance against a persistence model this project does not have — per-action state snapshots — and a shim nothing can exercise is a claim of compatibility nobody has tested.
