# Damage is assigned, then placed

The Rules Reference does not describe dealing damage as one act. It describes two numbered steps with a named window between them — `glossary/Dealing_Damage_Horror.md`: *"**1. Assign Damage/Horror:** Determine the amount of damage and/or horror being dealt. Place damage and/or horror tokens equal to the amount of damage and horror being dealt **next to** the cards that will be taking the damage/horror."*, then *"**2. Apply Damage/Horror:** Any assigned damage/horror that has not been prevented is now placed on each card to which it has been assigned, simultaneously."*, and between them *"Abilities that prevent, reduce, or reassign damage and/or horror that is being dealt are resolved between steps 1 and 2."*

The engine had step 1 as data and no step 1 as a *moment*. `Assignment` was already computed soak-first (or point by point on the interactive `DamageAssignment` frame), but the call that finished computing it also placed it: `place_and_emit_soak` ran `place_assignment` and only then announced anything. So there was no instant at which an assignment existed and no damage had landed, the between-steps window had nowhere to be, and the one condition the engine did emit — `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` — was necessarily **caller-owned** under ADR 0008, because the damage that triggered it had genuinely already been placed. Guard Dog 01021 prints *"[reaction] **When** an enemy attack deals damage to Guard Dog: Deal 1 damage to the attacking enemy."* (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/pack/core/core.json`) and was modelled `after`, which is the mis-tag #694 audited and #722 demanded back.

So **one deal of damage is two triggering conditions, the second nested inside the first**:

- **`DamageDealt`** is announced once the assignment exists, and is coordinator-owned. Its impact is the placement, so its resolve step emits `DamagePlaced` and that whole sequence runs between `DamageDealt`'s `when` and `at` cells. `glossary/When.md` puts a `when` *"immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition initiates, but before its impact upon the game state resolves"*, and `glossary/After.md` puts an `after` at the moment it *"has fully resolved"*. Dealing has not fully resolved until the harm is placed, since step 2 adds *"If no damage/horror is applied in this step, no damage/horror has been successfully dealt."* Its `when` cell is what Guard Dog prints, and a cancel there abandons the resolve step and so the placement. Its `after` cell is *"after you take"*. The Psychopomp's Song 02221 ruling draws exactly that line (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/02221>): its *"When you would take 1 or more damage"* is *"modifying the amount of damage you were about to take (but have not yet taken, as this is a "when..." effect)"*, and *"Note that this would be different if Psychopomp's Song read something like 'After you take 1 or more damage'"*.
- **`DamagePlaced`** is **coordinator-owned with a real impact**: `place_assignment`, unchanged, doing the simultaneous placement and the defeat determination that follows it. An ordinary migration in the shape of `EnemyAttacks` (#704) and `LeftLocation` (#721).

The full order of one deal is therefore: the assignment (the soak prompt, which is not a timing point), `DamageDealt`'s `when` cell, `DamagePlaced`'s `when` cell, the placement and defeat, `DamagePlaced`'s `at` and `after` cells, then `DamageDealt`'s `at` and `after` cells.

**Two conditions rather than one, because cards key off both steps and in both cells.** A sweep of all 159 Chapter 1 snapshot packs found them on either side of the window, and a single condition cannot hold both sets:

| | `when` cell | resolve | `after` cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DamageDealt` | Guard Dog 01021; Perseverance 04111 (*"Fast. Play when you are assigned damage and/or horror that would defeat you. / Cancel up to 4 of that damage and/or horror."*); Desiderio Delgado Álvarez 09607b (*"...exhaust him: Cancel 1 of that damage and deal 1 damage to the attacking enemy."*); Miskatonic Archaeology Funding 60232 (*"**Forced** - When you take damage or horror: No more than 1 of that damage/horror can be assigned to a [[Miskatonic]] asset."*) | `DamagePlaced`'s whole sequence | Psychosis 01099 (*"**Forced** - After you take 1 or more horror: Take 1 direct damage."*); Hypochondria 01100 (*"**Forced** - After you take 1 or more damage: Take 1 direct horror."*) |
| `DamagePlaced` | Baron Samedi 05019 (*"**Forced** - When any amount of damage is placed on an investigator in Baron Samedi's location: Place 1 additional damage on that investigator."*); Hank Samson 10016b (*"[reaction] When 1 or more damage is placed on you: Gain 2 resources."*) | `place_assignment` | Mark Harrigan 03001 (*"[reaction] After damage is placed on a card you control: Draw 1 card."*); Extraterrestrial Physiology 85007 (*"**Forced** - After any amount of damage is placed on Vulnerable Heart: Place twice that much damage on Subject 8L-08 as well."*); Detective Sherman 60177; Marie Lambeau 60451 |

Apart from Guard Dog, Psychosis and Hypochondria, which are implemented, none of these are in the Core or Dunwich corpus and none is implemented here. They are why the *shape* has to accommodate them: the snapshot is vendored as planning input precisely so this decision can be made against the full set of cards we will eventually support (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/SOURCE.md`). Baron Samedi is the one that forecloses the single-condition reading — a `when` cell on the *placement*, which must resolve after the assignment is settled and before the damage lands, so that the extra point joins it.

**The frame is `Continuation::DealDamage { investigator, source, direct, assignment, step }`**, walking `Distribute → Announce → Finish`. `Distribute` is the interactive per-point prompt (#44/K5b), which drains immediately when uncontested so the cursor always starts at the top (a `direct` deal offers no soaker, so it always drains: `glossary/Direct_Damage_Direct_Horror.md`, *"cannot be assigned or re-assigned elsewhere"*); `Announce` advances to `Finish` and emits `DamageDealt` in tail position, so the frame waits beneath that whole sequence, `DamagePlaced` included; `Finish` pops, resuming by source — `DamageSource::Effect` re-enters its effect walk, and the enemy-attack path simply pops so the `EnemyAttacks` coordinator runs its own `at` and `after` cells. A cancelled `DamageDealt` reaches `Finish` the same way, with nothing placed, so the frame needs no flag of its own. ADR 0003 forbids emitting from the middle of a call, so the cursor is not organisation: the assignment has to exist before the announcement, and the frame has to outlive the announcement's sequence to hand back to its caller.

**The frame owns the live assignment and each condition snapshots it into its event.** No write-back protocol exists, because the two emits happen at different moments: `Announce` carries the assignment as distribution left it, and `DamageDealt`'s resolve step re-reads the frame when it emits `DamagePlaced`, so that event carries whatever `DamageDealt`'s `when` cell made of it. Both events are true when emitted, which is the property that lets pattern matching keep reading the event rather than reaching down the stack — the self-binding that asked *is this card the event's `asset`* now asks *is this card a key in the event's `asset_damage`*.

**The worked example traces cell for cell**, which is the point of the shape and the integration test that pins it. `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md` runs its example on Guard Dog: an attack of opportunity assigns 1 damage to it, its `when` cell deals 1 damage to the Goat Spawn, that defeat's forced ability deals 1 horror to each investigator — a **nested deal with its own dealt/placed pair** — the horror lands at that nested deal's `DamagePlaced` resolve step, Agnes Baker 01004's *"After 1 or more horror is placed on Agnes Baker"* fires in its `after` cell, and only once everything unwinds does *"the players resolve the damage dealt to the Guard Dog"* — the original deal's `DamagePlaced`. Every sentence of that paragraph is a cell.

**It also fixes a live bug rather than only a tag.** `place_assignment` returned only the damaged assets that *survived*, and those were what got announced, so a Guard Dog killed by the attack that damaged it never retaliated — against its own ruling, `data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01021.md`: *"You can use Guard Dog's ability when you assign lethal damage/horror to it."* Announcing the assignment before anything is placed makes the survivor filter unnecessary and the ruling true. (The card was later reprinted as **09034** with *"When an enemy attack deals damage **and/or horror** to Guard Dog"*; FFG having to widen the wording is what settles that 01021 as printed triggers on damage only, and that the ruling's "/horror" is about lethality rather than about the trigger.)

## Considered options

**Keeping the placement with the attack and making the soak condition a bare milestone** — one condition, announced per damaged asset, with the whole simultaneous placement parked on a frame that runs after every announcement's sequence — was the first proposal and reads well until the `after` cell is asked about. It would run *"after an enemy attack deals damage to X"* before the damage was placed, and it had to argue that a condition whose printed name is *"deals damage"* has no impact of its own. The argument was available (the placement belongs to the frame that emitted, which is the bare-milestone test ADR 0008 already applies to `RoundEnded`) but it was an argument, where splitting the conditions and nesting the placement inside the deal gives *"deals damage"* the placement as its impact outright. It also announced N nested sequences for N damaged assets, which models as sequential a thing the rules call simultaneous.

**Placing each asset's share in its own condition's resolve step** was rejected on the clause it contradicts. It reproduces the `Nested_Sequences.md` ordering exactly for the single-asset case, but it takes one card's damage out of *"placed on each card to which it has been assigned, simultaneously"*, splits that card's own damage from its own horror across two moments, and leaves a partial cancel — Desiderio's *"Cancel 1 of that damage"* — with nothing to edit but the whole-condition cancellation flag, which has no vocabulary for *one* of the damage.

**One condition carrying the whole assignment** was the strongest rejected option, and it fails only on the window. It gets `when` and `after` right for most of the sweep, but the Rules Reference puts a named step between assignment and application, and Baron Samedi 05019 and Perseverance 04111 sit on opposite sides of it. Collapsing them would make a card that interrupts *placement* resolve in the same cell as one that interrupts *assignment*.

## Consequences

**A soak reaction now precedes the damage it reacts to, in the event log.** Guard Dog's `EnemyDamaged` lands before the investigator's `DamagePlacedOnInvestigator` for the same attack, where it used to follow it. This is the visible shape of the fix, and the enemy-attack event-order tests assert it.

**Every investigator harm rides the frame.** An enemy attack begins its deal directly; everything else — `Effect::Deal`, and the harm a native or the engine deals outside the DSL (Crypt Chill 01167's fallback, the deck-out horror, Dynamite Blast 01024) — begins one through `deal_harm` with `DamageSource::Effect`. So every harm offers the soak and announces both conditions, and an ability keyed to either fires whatever dealt it. `deal_harm` is tail position like any emit: a caller with work after the harm parks it on a frame beneath (ADR 0003). Dynamite Blast deals each investigator at the chosen location separately, pushing one frame per investigator in reverse id order so the deals resolve ascending, each — its soak prompt, both conditions, any reaction — completing before the next frame is exposed.

**Editing an assignment is not the prevention signal, and the engine cannot yet tell them apart.** Desiderio's *"Cancel 1 of that damage"* leaves the condition to occur with one card's share smaller; if that share reaches zero the card stops being a valid candidate for a window that is already open, which is a **withdrawal** (`withdraw_suppressed_candidates`, #714) rather than an abandoned sequence. The prevention flag (`EmitEventFrame::prevented`, ADR 0008) says only *prevented*, so this is **#366**'s to answer — as is the narrow staleness that comes with the snapshot rule above: an `after DamageDealt` ability reading a share that a `when`-cell ability shrank would read the value the event was emitted with. `HarmTaken` is such a reader: it matches the `DamageDealt` event's assignment as announced, so a `when`-cell ability that reduced a share to 0 without cancelling would still fire an *"after you take"* Forced such as Psychosis 01099's. Psychopomp's Song 02221 (phase 10) is the first card to edit the amount in that window (*"When you would take 1 or more damage: Take 2 additional damage"*). No corpus card edits an assignment, so both are recorded and neither is built.

**A full cancel in `DamageDealt`'s `when` cell stops the placement; a cancel in `DamagePlaced`'s does not stop `DamageDealt`'s `after` cell.** `#714`'s prevention flag sits on the cancelled condition's own coordinator frame (ADR 0008), and the resolve step it abandons is, for `DamageDealt`, the placement — Perseverance 04111's *"Cancel up to 4 of that damage and/or horror"* works with no flag on the deal. A cancel one level down, in the nested `DamagePlaced`'s `when` cell, abandons only the placement: `DamageDealt`'s `at` and `after` cells still run, reading `DamageDealt`'s pre-placement assignment, so *"after you take"* would fire on harm that was never placed. No corpus card cancels there (Baron Samedi 05019, the only `when` on it in the sweep, adds damage rather than cancelling). Telling the outer condition that its impact was prevented is **#366**'s, along with partial replacement, since both want the same vocabulary: the flag says only *prevented*, and these need to say *prevented, and how much*.

**A prevented harm cost leaves the ability unpaid, and the engine would resolve it anyway.** `Cost::TakeHarm` deals its harm on this frame with the ability's effect parked beneath (#1029), gated only on the controller still being `Active`. Jim Culver 02004's ruling makes prevention the line: *"as long as that damage isn’t prevented or canceled, the cost is considered paid and he can resolve the rest of that ally’s ability"* (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/02004>). A cancel in `DamageDealt`'s `when` cell now reaches the deal and leaves nothing placed, but the parked effect is not told its cost went unpaid, and resolves anyway. No corpus card cancels harm, so nothing triggers it. It is **#366**'s along with the nested cancel above, since both want the deal's caller to know it was prevented.

**A move is neither a deal nor a heal, so it is its own exit.** The official FAQ rules out both halves. On the heal side: *"Q: Does an effect that moves damage/horror from one card to another count as 'healing' the damage or horror? A: No. Moving tokens does not count as healing for the purpose of card effects."* On the deal side, The Necronomicon 01009's own ruling says the same from the other end: *"Moving damage or horror is different from 'dealing' or 'taking' damage or horror, so does not trigger effects […]"* (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01009>). So a move does not run this ADR's assign-then-place procedure at all: there is nothing to assign, no between-steps window, and no `DamageDealt`/`DamagePlaced` pair. The FAQ adds one destination constraint: *"An effect that can 'move' damage/horror from one card to another … must move that damage/horror to a card that has a health or sanity value, respectively."* Both quotes are from `data/official-faq/Frequently_Asked_Questions.md`. The Necronomicon is the first card to move horror, and it does so through `NativeCx::move_horror` (`dispatch/tokens.rs`, `move_horror`). The exit decrements one card and increments another, emits only `Event::HorrorMoved`, refuses a destination with no sanity value, and then defeats a destination the horror reached, as any horror on it would. `Seq[Heal, Deal]` remains the wrong encoding for the next move card, because `Effect::Heal` welds the decrement to `Event::Healed` (`evaluator.rs`, `heal_effect`) and `Deal` runs this procedure. No corpus card moves damage, so the damage twin waits for its card.

**`DamagePlaced`'s pattern is `HarmPlaced`, and it reads a share of the placement.** Agnes Baker 01004 is its first card: *"After 1 or more horror is placed on Agnes Baker"*. `HarmPlaced { kind, scope }` requires at least 1 of `kind` on the card `scope` names, read from the event's assignment. Today the only scope is the controller's investigator card. Reading the share rather than the event matters because the event fires even when a soaker absorbed every point, and her ruling is *"You cannot use Agnes' ability if you assign horror to an asset."* The wider "taken by you", which counts the assets too (`GLOSSARY.md`), is not a scope of `HarmPlaced` but a pattern of its own, `HarmTaken`, read in `DamageDealt`'s `after` cell once the nested placement has resolved. Psychosis 01099 is its first card: *"**Forced** - After you take 1 or more horror: Take 1 direct damage."* Neither condition needs anything else to reach a forced ability: the one trigger scan ([ADR 0018](0018-one-trigger-scan-walks-the-whole-board.md)) walks the board for forced and reaction abilities alike at every condition, so a forced `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` would be collected at `DamageDealt` as soon as a card printed one.

**Two damaged soakers are two candidates in one cell, not two nested sequences.** That makes their order a question about ordering simultaneous triggers, which is **#213**, rather than a push order maintained by the emitting call site — which is where it belonged all along, and which the rules agree with: one deal, one moment.

**`Continuation::DamageAssignment` is gone, and the rename is wire-visible without being a break.** Continuation frames reach clients inside serialized game state, so the rename looks like a break. It is the same question ADR 0008 answered for the `bucket` → `step` rename, with the same answer — no persisted artifact can hold a frame of either name, on the seed-plus-`ResolveInput`-log argument ADR 0008 states in full from `crates/server/src/session.rs` — and, deliberately, the same absence of a compatibility shim.
