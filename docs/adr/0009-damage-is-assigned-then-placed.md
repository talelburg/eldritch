# Damage is assigned, then placed

The Rules Reference does not describe dealing damage as one act. It describes two numbered steps with a named window between them — `glossary/Dealing_Damage_Horror.md`: *"**1. Assign Damage/Horror:** Determine the amount of damage and/or horror being dealt. Place damage and/or horror tokens equal to the amount of damage and horror being dealt **next to** the cards that will be taking the damage/horror."*, then *"**2. Apply Damage/Horror:** Any assigned damage/horror that has not been prevented is now placed on each card to which it has been assigned, simultaneously."*, and between them *"Abilities that prevent, reduce, or reassign damage and/or horror that is being dealt are resolved between steps 1 and 2."*

The engine had step 1 as data and no step 1 as a *moment*. `Assignment` was already computed soak-first (or point by point on the interactive `DamageAssignment` frame), but the call that finished computing it also placed it: `place_and_emit_soak` ran `place_assignment` and only then announced anything. So there was no instant at which an assignment existed and no damage had landed, the between-steps window had nowhere to be, and the one condition the engine did emit — `EnemyAttackDamagedSelf` — was necessarily **caller-owned** under ADR 0008, because the damage that triggered it had genuinely already been placed. Guard Dog 01021 prints *"[reaction] **When** an enemy attack deals damage to Guard Dog: Deal 1 damage to the attacking enemy."* (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/pack/core/core.json`) and was modelled `after`, which is the mis-tag #694 audited and #722 demanded back.

So **one deal of damage is two triggering conditions**, sequenced by one continuation frame:

- **`DamageAssigned`** is a **bare milestone** in ADR 0008's sense — coordinator-owned with a documented no-op resolve step. Nothing about the board changes when an assignment is made: the assignment is in-flight data on the frame, and the only mutation anywhere in the procedure is the placement, which belongs to the other condition. Its `when` cell is what Guard Dog prints.
- **`DamagePlaced`** is **coordinator-owned with a real impact**: `place_assignment`, unchanged, doing the simultaneous placement and the defeat determination that follows it. An ordinary migration in the shape of `EnemyAttacks` (#704) and `LeftLocation` (#721).

**Two conditions rather than one, because cards key off both steps and in both cells.** A sweep of all 159 Chapter 1 snapshot packs found them on either side of the window, and a single condition cannot hold both sets:

| | `when` cell | resolve | `after` cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DamageAssigned` | Guard Dog 01021; Perseverance 04111 (*"Fast. Play when you are assigned damage and/or horror that would defeat you. / Cancel up to 4 of that damage and/or horror."*); Desiderio Delgado Álvarez 09607b (*"...exhaust him: Cancel 1 of that damage and deal 1 damage to the attacking enemy."*); Miskatonic Archaeology Funding 60232 (*"**Forced** - When you take damage or horror: No more than 1 of that damage/horror can be assigned to a [[Miskatonic]] asset."*) | — | (no takers) |
| `DamagePlaced` | Baron Samedi 05019 (*"**Forced** - When any amount of damage is placed on an investigator in Baron Samedi's location: Place 1 additional damage on that investigator."*); Hank Samson 10016b (*"[reaction] When 1 or more damage is placed on you: Gain 2 resources."*) | `place_assignment` | Mark Harrigan 03001 (*"[reaction] After damage is placed on a card you control: Draw 1 card."*); Vulnerable Heart 85043 (*"**Forced** - After **any amount** of damage is placed on Vulnerable Heart: Place twice that much damage on Subject 8L-08 as well."*); Detective Sherman 60177; Marie Lambeau 60451 |

None of these are in the Core or Dunwich corpus and none are implemented here. They are why the *shape* has to accommodate them: the snapshot is vendored as planning input precisely so this decision can be made against the full set of cards we will eventually support (`data/arkhamdb-snapshot/SOURCE.md`). Baron Samedi is the one that forecloses the single-condition reading — a `when` cell on the *placement*, which must resolve after the assignment is settled and before the damage lands, so that the extra point joins it.

**The frame is `Continuation::DealDamage { investigator, source, assignment, step }`**, walking `Distribute → Announce → Place → Finish`. `Distribute` is the interactive per-point prompt (#44/K5b), which drains immediately when uncontested so the cursor always starts at the top; `Announce` emits `DamageAssigned` in tail position and advances; `Place` emits `DamagePlaced` in tail position and advances; `Finish` pops, resuming by source — `DamageSource::Effect` re-enters its effect walk, and the enemy-attack path simply pops so the `EnemyAttacks` coordinator runs its own `at` and `after` cells. Two emits with a live object between them is exactly the manoeuvre ADR 0003 forbids doing synchronously, so the cursor is not organisation; it is the only legal way to sequence them.

**The frame owns the live assignment and each emit snapshots it into its event.** No write-back protocol exists, because the two emits happen at different steps: `Announce` carries the assignment as distribution left it, and `Place` re-reads the frame, so it carries whatever `DamageAssigned`'s cells made of it. Both events are true when emitted, which is the property that lets pattern matching keep reading the event rather than reaching down the stack — the self-binding that asked *is this card the event's `asset`* now asks *is this card a key in the event's `asset_damage`*.

**The worked example traces cell for cell**, which is the point of the shape and the integration test that pins it. `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md` runs its example on Guard Dog: an attack of opportunity assigns 1 damage to it, its `when` cell deals 1 damage to the Goat Spawn, that defeat's forced ability deals 1 horror to each investigator — a **nested deal with its own assigned/placed pair** — the horror lands at that nested deal's `DamagePlaced` resolve step, Agnes Baker 01004's *"After 1 or more horror is placed on Agnes Baker"* fires in its `after` cell, and only once everything unwinds does *"the players resolve the damage dealt to the Guard Dog"* — the original deal's `DamagePlaced`. Every sentence of that paragraph is a cell.

**It also fixes a live bug rather than only a tag.** `place_assignment` returned only the damaged assets that *survived*, and those were what got announced, so a Guard Dog killed by the attack that damaged it never retaliated — against its own ruling, `data/arkhamdb-faq/core/01021.md`: *"You can use Guard Dog's ability when you assign lethal damage/horror to it."* Announcing the assignment before anything is placed makes the survivor filter unnecessary and the ruling true. (The card was later reprinted as **09034** with *"When an enemy attack deals damage **and/or horror** to Guard Dog"*; FFG having to widen the wording is what settles that 01021 as printed triggers on damage only, and that the ruling's "/horror" is about lethality rather than about the trigger.)

## Considered options

**Keeping the placement with the attack and making the soak condition a bare milestone** — one condition, announced per damaged asset, with the whole simultaneous placement parked on a frame that runs after every announcement's sequence — was the first proposal and reads well until the `after` cell is asked about. It would run *"after an enemy attack deals damage to X"* before the damage was placed, and it had to argue that a condition whose printed name is *"deals damage"* has no impact of its own. The argument was available (the placement belongs to the frame that emitted, which is the bare-milestone test ADR 0008 already applies to `RoundEnded`) but it was an argument, where splitting the conditions makes the emptiness of `DamageAssigned`'s resolve step simply true. It also announced N nested sequences for N damaged assets, which models as sequential a thing the rules call simultaneous.

**Placing each asset's share in its own condition's resolve step** was rejected on the clause it contradicts. It reproduces the `Nested_Sequences.md` ordering exactly for the single-asset case, but it takes one card's damage out of *"placed on each card to which it has been assigned, simultaneously"*, splits that card's own damage from its own horror across two moments, and leaves a partial cancel — Desiderio's *"Cancel 1 of that damage"* — with nothing to edit but the whole-condition cancellation bool, which has no vocabulary for *one* of the damage.

**One condition carrying the whole assignment** was the strongest rejected option, and it fails only on the window. It gets `when` and `after` right for most of the sweep, but the Rules Reference puts a named step between assignment and application, and Baron Samedi 05019 and Perseverance 04111 sit on opposite sides of it. Collapsing them would make a card that interrupts *placement* resolve in the same cell as one that interrupts *assignment*.

## Consequences

**A soak reaction now precedes the damage it reacts to, in the event log.** Guard Dog's `EnemyDamaged` lands before the investigator's `DamageTaken` for the same attack, where it used to follow it. This is the visible shape of the fix, and the enemy-attack event-order tests assert it.

**Two placement entry points do not conform, and the gap is tracked rather than hidden.** `take_damage` and `take_horror` reach placement through `soak_and_place` and place inline, so they announce nothing — recorded as **#728**, whose real cost is Dynamite Blast 01024's `for inv in investigators` loop, which needs parking on a frame before it can queue abilities per iteration (#704's shape, in miniature). Until then a future ability keyed to either condition fires for an enemy attack and for `Effect::Deal` but not for those two. Nothing in the Core or Dunwich corpus observes it, and migrating call sites one at a time with the classification making the gap visible is the discipline ADR 0008 already runs on.

**Editing an assignment is not the prevention signal, and the engine cannot yet tell them apart.** Desiderio's *"Cancel 1 of that damage"* leaves the condition to occur with one card's share smaller; if that share reaches zero the card stops being a valid candidate for a window that is already open, which is a **withdrawal** (`withdraw_suppressed_candidates`, #714) rather than an abandoned sequence. `pending_cancellation` is one bool and says only *prevented*, so this is **#366**'s to answer — as is the narrow staleness that comes with the snapshot rule above: an `after DamageAssigned` ability reading a share that a `when`-cell ability shrank would read the value the event was emitted with. No corpus card edits an assignment, so both are recorded and neither is built.

**A full cancel in `DamageAssigned`'s `when` cell would abandon its sequence but not the deal.** `#714`'s prevention signal pops the coordinator (`abandon_emit`) and is consumed at the resolve step, so the `DealDamage` frame parked beneath it — which never sees the signal — would advance to `Place` and place the assignment anyway. Nothing reaches it today: `Effect::Cancel` is the only setter and Dodge 01023, the only corpus card with one, declares `EnemyAttacks`, whose cancel is caught one level up and never reaches the deal at all. Perseverance 04111's *"Cancel up to 4 of that damage and/or horror"* is the card that would contest it, and it is outside the corpus. Giving the frame the signal is **#366**'s to do along with partial replacement, since the two want the same vocabulary: the bool says only *prevented*, and this needs to say *prevented, and here is whose*.

**`DamagePlaced` gets no `EventPattern` and neither condition gets a forced point.** The condition is load-bearing — it is what places the damage — but a card-facing pattern with no corpus declaration is decoration, and `forced_point()` returning `Some` with nothing to scan is dead code. Both wait for a card, on the same threshold that keeps DSL primitives out until two of them want the same one.

**Two damaged soakers are two candidates in one cell, not two nested sequences.** That makes their order a question about ordering simultaneous triggers, which is **#213**, rather than a push order maintained by the emitting call site — which is where it belonged all along, and which the rules agree with: one deal, one moment.

**`Continuation::DamageAssignment` is gone, and the rename is wire-visible without being a break.** Continuation frames reach clients inside serialized game state, but the server persists a seed state plus a `ResolveInput`-only action log (`crates/server/src/session.rs`) and replays it on load; the seed is post-seating and mulligan-pending, so no persisted artifact can hold a frame of either name. This is the same question ADR 0008 answered for the `bucket` → `step` rename, with the same answer and, deliberately, the same absence of a compatibility shim.
