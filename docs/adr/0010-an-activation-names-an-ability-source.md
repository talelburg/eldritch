# An activation names an ability source

The Rules Reference says which sources an investigator may use a triggered ability from, and it says it once for `[free]`, `[reaction]` and `[action]` alike — `glossary/Triggered_Abilities.md`, verbatim:

> An investigator is permitted to use triggered abilities (\[free\], \[reaction\], and \[action\] abilities) from the following sources:
>
> - A card in play and under his or her control. This includes his or her investigator card.
> - A scenario card that is in play and at the same location as the investigator. This includes the location itself, encounter cards placed at that location, and all encounter cards in the threat area of any investigator at that location.
> - The current act or current agenda card.
> - Any card that explicitly allows the investigator to activate its ability.

The engine honoured one of the four, and only partly. Activation named a bare `CardInstanceId` and resolved it against a single collection — the acting investigator's `cards_in_play` — so an ability on your **own investigator card** or on a card in your **own threat area** was refused on submission and never offered in the turn menu, even though both are cards in play under your control by the first bullet's own second sentence. Meanwhile the forced and reaction scans walk `Investigator::controlled_card_instances`, which has covered all three collections since #448, and address every source through `CandidateSource`. The same card was half-reachable: an ability on it could fire, but a player could not initiate it. **Two paths, two answers to "a card you control"** — which is the disagreement `CONTEXT.md`'s bar for a glossary entry asks for, and why **ability source** is now defined there.

So **the activation action stops naming a card instance and names an ability source**: `TurnAction::ActivateAbility { investigator, source: AbilitySource, ability_index }`, with the descriptor carried through the parked `ActionResume::ActivateAbility` frame as well.

**An in-play card instance means any card instance in play, wherever it sits.** `AbilitySource::InPlay` names an instance and says nothing about which collection holds it — investigator card, cards in play, threat area, and later a location's or an enemy's attachments all carry an instance identity already. Whether *this* investigator can reach *that* instance is a separate question with a separate answer. Keeping zone membership out of the addressing vocabulary is what stops the four bullets becoming a pile of special cases, and it is what lets the descriptor grow (a location and an enemy kind with #708, an act and an agenda kind with #709) without any call site learning a new way to say "the source".

**One reachability predicate implements the bullets, and it is defined as an enumeration.** `engine::ability_source::reachable_sources` lists what an investigator can reach; `resolve` is a lookup *in that list*, not a second scan. The turn-menu enumerator lists from the first and the validator asks the second, so the menu and handler-acceptance cannot drift into disagreeing about which sources exist — the same by-construction property `check_activate_ability` already gave them for legality. `enumerate_fast_plays` consults it too, because the bullets are written once for all three ability icons and the fast window is the `[free]` half of the same sentence. This slice implements the control bullet; #708 and #709 add co-location and the board cards to the same function.

**Widening which sources are addressable does not widen what is legal.** Everything `Appendix_I_Initiation_Sequence.md` requires still runs, unchanged, after reachability has answered — *"Check play restrictions: determine if the card can be played, or if the ability can be initiated, at this time. (This includes verifying that the resolution of the effect has the potential to change the game state.)"*, and that the cost *"can be paid"*. An unpayable cost and a provably inert effect stay unoffered from every reachable source, which is what the integration test asserts source by source.

**Cost payment goes through the same predicate.** `require_source_reachable` re-resolves the source at the moment each cost is paid (#706's identity-addressing contract), so a cost cannot reach a card the activation was never allowed to name, and a source removed by an earlier cost is simply absent rather than a stale position pointing at a neighbour. `resolve_mut` decides *reachability* by calling `resolve` and only then re-finds the instance mutably — it never re-derives the bullets, which is the difference between one predicate and two that agree by luck until #708 makes a co-located threat area reachable by a non-controller.

## Considered options

**Widening the existing lookup zone by zone** — keep the bare `CardInstanceId` and have the validator search one more collection. This is the original framing of the problem, and Haunted 01098's ruling kills it (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01098>), verbatim:

> Any investigator at the same location as the investigator with Haunted in their threat area may trigger the \[action\]\[action\] to discard Haunted, as per the FAQ \[V1.0, section 2.1\].

matching the printed bullet's *"all encounter cards in the threat area of **any** investigator at that location"*. The answer is therefore N investigators' collections selected by co-location, not one more collection on the acting investigator. It also cannot reach locations, acts or agendas at all, since none of them carries a card instance to name — so it fixes one bullet and leaves three, which is exactly how the current shape arose in the first place.

**Giving locations, acts and agendas a card-instance identity**, so the action's payload need not change at all. This dovetails with #290, and it is the option that keeps the wire still. It was rejected because it hands locations and enemies two identities apiece, and every call site that looks them up — which is most of the engine, by `LocationId` and `EnemyId` — then has to pick which one it means. `CandidateSource`'s own documentation already refused this once, for `CandidateSource::Location`: *"Locations have a `LocationId`, not a `CardInstanceId`, so this can't fold into `InPlay` (#553)."*

**One entity table with capability facets** — every board object in a single table, with abilities as one facet among many; the 2026-08-14 forward-compatibility register's radical option 4.4. Rejected because Chapter 1 *content* does not force it: 4.4's three motivating cases are Innsmouth and Hemlock Vale, years out, and the typed partition of the board survives this feature untouched. The descriptor is a vocabulary for *addressing* sources, which is what the rules bullets are about; it is not a claim that locations, enemies and cards are the same kind of thing.

## Consequences

**This is a wire break with no migration, and persisted games are discarded rather than migrated.** Sessions are reconstructed by deserializing a frozen seed state and replaying a `ResolveInput`-only action log over it, and there is no schema version anywhere in the protocol — so a seed whose continuation stack holds a parked `ActionResume::ActivateAbility { instance_id }` frame no longer deserializes. Nothing has shipped, so old games are deleted. **No version field is added here**: versioning is its own decision and belongs with #581.

**The server now fails loudly on a log it cannot replay.** Two gaps made a stale log look like something else. `GameSession::load` ignored each replayed action's outcome, so a logged action that the engine now *rejects* produced a session reconstructed from the prefix that still replayed — a silently wrong board rather than an error; it returns `SessionError::Replay { seq, reason }` instead. And `get_or_load_room` collapsed every load error into `None`, reporting an unreplayable game as a nonexistent one; it now logs at `error` and refuses to attach. This matters more than the payload change itself, because the log addresses the turn menu **by position**: an `OptionId` deserializes forever, and the action it selects is whatever the enumerator lists today.

**`AbilitySource` has one variant today, and that is deliberate.** A single-variant enum earns its keep by being the place the other three kinds land without touching a call site, and by making the question it answers — *which source*, not *which collection* — nameable in code. #708 and #709 are the tickets that fill it in.

**A cost that discards its source now routes by zone, because widening the source set made a panic reachable.** `discard_card_from_play` panics on an instance that is not in `cards_in_play`, and a `Cost::DiscardSelf` on a threat-area card would have reached it. A **panic reachable from player input must not ship**, so `discard_source` sends a card in play to its controller's discard pile and a threat-area card to the encounter discard (the existing `discard_from_threat_area`), and rejects cleanly for the one source that cannot be discarded at all — the investigator card. No corpus card prints such a cost; the rejection is deliberately unlifted with no tracking issue, as with `reject_incompatible_costs`.

**`Event::AbilityActivated` still carries a `CardInstanceId`, and the growth is a compile error rather than a panic.** Every source kind in scope names an instance, so the field is filled by destructuring — `let AbilitySource::InPlay(instance_id) = source;` in the handler, and an exhaustive `match` in `TurnAction::target`. Both stop compiling the day #709 adds a kind with no instance, which is when the event's field has to become optional and the option's anchor has to be decided. The alternative shapes — an `expect` on `source.instance()`, or a `map_or` fallback — would have made the same day a panic reachable from player input, or a menu entry silently anchored to nothing.

## Amendment (#708)

Two consequences above were written as predictions and have now been settled, earlier and differently than they anticipated. The decision itself is unchanged; what follows records what actually landed.

**The enum has three variants, and the second bullet is implemented.** #708 added `Location(LocationId)` and `Enemy(EnemyId)` and taught `reachable_sources` the co-location bullet — the location itself, the encounter cards placed on it (its attachments and the enemies standing there, with theirs), and the threat areas of *any* investigator at that location, which is the not-controller-scoped half Haunted 01098's ruling settles. No call site learned a new way to say "the source", which is the property the single-variant enum was holding open. #709 still owns the act and agenda kinds.

**`Event::AbilityActivated` names the source, not an optional instance.** The prediction was that #709 would force the field to become `Option<CardInstanceId>` and that "the option's anchor has to be decided". #708 forced it first — a location has no `CardInstanceId` — and the anchor question is what settled the shape: an `Option<CardInstanceId>` answers *"which instance, if any"* and throws away *which location*, so a consumer asking what carried the ability would have to reconstruct it from the code plus the board. The field is therefore the whole `AbilitySource`, and a consumer that specifically wants an instance calls `source.instance()` and handles the `None`. The compile-error property the original paragraph was protecting is preserved: `TurnAction::target`'s `match` is still exhaustive, so #709's kinds cannot land silently anchored to nothing.

**A usage limit on a source with no instance rejects, naming #699.** Usage state is `CardInPlay::ability_usage`, per-instance, so a limited location ability had nowhere to record a use and reached `bump_usage_counter`'s `unreachable!`. #708 is what first put that branch behind player input, and **a panic reachable from player input must not ship** — same reasoning as the discard-by-zone consequence above. A source-referencing cost (`Exhaust`, `SpendUses`, `DiscardSelf`) on such a source rejects for the same reason, at validation rather than mid-payment, so the turn menu never offers one. No corpus card prints either; #699 builds the capability the Dunwich locations will need.

## Amendment (#709)

**The enum is complete for the bullets it implements, and the last two kinds carry no id.** `Act` and `Agenda` name *the current* act and agenda, resolved through `act_deck[act_index]` and `agenda_deck[agenda_index]` at the moment the source is looked up. The alternative — naming the board card by cursor position, or by its printed code — would let an action address an act that is no longer current, and would put "is this still the current one" in every caller. Naming the cursor instead is what makes *"an ability on an act that is no longer current is not reachable"* true by construction: there is nothing else for the descriptor to point at. `CandidateSource::Board`, which does carry a code, has to compare it against the current act and agenda at every read (`candidate_anchor`, `candidate_source_present`) — that comparison is the shape this avoids.

**The third bullet is gated on nothing, and the co-location pass had to move to say so.** `reachable_sources` returned early for an investigator standing at no location, which was correct while co-location was the last bullet. It is not correct with a bullet below it that does not depend on standing anywhere — Disrupting the Ritual 01148's ruling settles that for a printed card (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01148>), verbatim:

> Your investigator doesn't need to be at the Ritual Site in order to activate the ability of this act card.

So the co-location walk became `colocated_sources`, returning its own list, and the two bullets are independent rather than sequential. An early `return` in a function body implementing three independent rules is a bug waiting for the fourth.

**The two descriptors did not converge, and that is the part of this ADR that was wrong.** The original text said `CandidateSource` and `AbilitySource` "converge when those kinds land", and #709 was written to split `CandidateSource::Board` into an act kind and an agenda kind. `Board` turns out to carry a third thing: `forced_triggers.rs`'s `EnemyAttacks` arm pushes the attacking **enemy's own** ability under it, and `candidate_anchor` falls through to `OptionTarget::Global` for that case rather than naming the enemy. Splitting `Board` into two therefore needs a third kind — `Enemy(EnemyId)` — which #709 neither names nor needs: all six of its acceptance criteria hold without touching `CandidateSource`, because the descriptor an *action* names is `AbilitySource`, and it does distinguish act from agenda. **#735** owns the convergence, and it inherits the question this ADR assumed away: whether the two enums merge at all, given that `CandidateSource` has a `Hand` kind (a Fast event played from hand, which is not an ability source in the rules' sense) and `AbilitySource` does not.

## Amendment (#735)

**The two descriptors converged, and the shape of the convergence is a wrapper rather than a merge.** The #709 amendment above left one question open: whether `CandidateSource` and `AbilitySource` become one enum, given that the former has a `Hand` kind the latter does not. They do not merge. `CandidateSource` is now

```rust
pub enum CandidateSource {
    Ability(AbilitySource),
    Hand,
}
```

so **where an ability comes from is said in exactly one vocabulary**, and the one thing outside that vocabulary is the one thing that is not an ability source: a Fast event played from hand. `glossary/Triggered_Abilities.md`'s bullets, quoted at the top of this ADR, list the sources of *triggered abilities* — none of them is a card in hand, and a hand event is **played** (RR Appendix I: `CardPlayed`, run the ability, discard) rather than fired in place. Folding `Hand` into `AbilitySource` would have put a kind into the enum that `reachable_sources` enumerates which can never be reachable, and would have made the rules' own list stop being what the type means.

**`Board` had been carrying three things, which is why it could not simply split in two.** #695 called for an act kind and an agenda kind. `forced_triggers.rs`'s `EnemyAttacks` arm pushes the attacking **enemy's own** ability under `Board` as well — Silver Twilight Acolyte 01102's *"**Forced** - After Silver Twilight Acolyte attacks: Place 1 doom on the current agenda."* — so the split needed an enemy kind too, which `AbilitySource` already had.

**One map from a source to a board anchor, in one place.** `impl From<AbilitySource> for OptionTarget`. The turn menu (`TurnAction::target`) and the forced / reaction options (`candidate_anchor`) each carried a copy, and the copies had drifted: the candidate side worked out *which* board card a `Board` candidate was by comparing its code against the current act and agenda, and fell through to `OptionTarget::Global` when neither matched — which is precisely what an attacking enemy's own ability hit, so 01102's acknowledge prompt anchored to nothing. **`Global` is no longer any candidate's anchor.** The exhaustiveness the old `match` was protecting — a new source kind must break the build rather than quietly anchor itself somewhere wrong — now sits in the `From` impl, where breaking it once breaks both paths.

**Existence and reachability are two questions, and the code says which is which.** `ability_source::source_card` returns the card a source names right now, board-wide and ungated, and sits directly beside `reachable_sources` with each doc comment pointing at the other. The `LapseReason::SourceGone` probe asks the first, deliberately: **a forced ability is not restricted to the sources its controller could legally *use***, so routing it through reachability would report a still-present enemy as gone the moment the investigator it attacked moved away. Returning the card rather than a bool is what lets the probe ask the sharper question in one step — *still there **and** still the card this candidate was minted from* — which `Act` and `Agenda` need, since those name **the current** one: an advanced act has not vanished, it has become a different card, and a candidate minted from the old one has lapsed either way. No caller re-derives which board card a source is.

**Two lapse *attributions* change, and no resolution does.** The probe is board-wide and code-comparing where the old per-kind arms were controller-scoped and code-blind, so two candidates that used to be labelled `LapseReason::SourceGone` are now labelled by whichever gate actually withdrew them: an attacking enemy's own ability (its code matched neither board card, so the old arm always said "gone"), and a candidate minted from a location's or an enemy's **attachment** — Barricade 01038's `LeftLocation` self-discard is one — which no investigator's collections hold. `LapseReason` is *"best-effort attribution … for the client log"* and a mislabel there is cosmetic; **which candidates survive a re-scan is decided by `withdraw_lapsed_candidates`, not by this probe**, and that is untouched. The forced and reaction paths resolve exactly as before, which the corpus integration tests assert unchanged.

**The usage-limit panic stays a panic.** `bump_usage_counter`'s `unreachable!` now covers "an ability source with no card instance behind it" as one clause instead of three kinds, and names #699 — the ticket that builds the state-level counter — the way the activation-side `reject_untrackable_usage_limit` already does. Building a second untrackable-limit gate on the forced path would duplicate what #699 replaces, and no corpus card prints a usage-limited forced ability on a location, an enemy, the act or the agenda.

**Same wire posture as #707 and #709.** `CandidateSource` rides serialized game state inside a `ResolutionCandidate` on an open window frame; the nesting changes that payload, persisted games are discarded rather than migrated, and no version field is added (that is #581).
