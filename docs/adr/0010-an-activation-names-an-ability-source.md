# An activation names an ability source

The Rules Reference says which sources an investigator may use a triggered ability from, and it says it once for `[free]`, `[reaction]` and `[action]` alike — `glossary/Triggered_Abilities.md`, verbatim:

> An investigator is permitted to use triggered abilities (\[free\], \[reaction\], and \[action\] abilities) from the following sources:
>
> - A card in play and under his or her control. This includes his or her investigator card.
> - A scenario card that is in play and at the same location as the investigator. This includes the location itself, encounter cards placed at that location, and all encounter cards in the threat area of any investigator at that location.
> - The current act or current agenda card.
> - Any card that explicitly allows the investigator to activate its ability.

The engine honoured one of the four, and only partly. Activation named a bare `CardInstanceId` and resolved it against a single collection — the acting investigator's `cards_in_play` — so an ability on your **own investigator card** or on a card in your **own threat area** was refused on submission and never offered in the turn menu, even though both are cards in play under your control by the first bullet's own second sentence. Meanwhile the forced and reaction scans walk `Investigator::controlled_card_instances`, which has covered all three collections since #448, and address every source through `CandidateSource`. The same card was half-reachable: an ability on it could fire, but a player could not initiate it. **Two paths, two answers to "a card you control"** — which is the disagreement `CONTEXT.md`'s bar for a glossary entry asks for, and why **ability source** is now defined there.

So **the activation action stops naming a card instance and names an ability source**: `TurnAction::ActivateAbility { investigator, source: AbilitySource, ability_index }`, with the descriptor carried through the parked `ActionResume::ActivateAbility` frame as well.

**An in-play card instance means any card instance in play, wherever it sits.** `AbilitySource::InPlay` names an instance and says nothing about which collection holds it — investigator card, cards in play, threat area, and later a location's or an enemy's attachments all carry an instance identity already. Whether *this* investigator can reach *that* instance is a separate question with a separate answer. Keeping zone membership out of the addressing vocabulary is what stops the four bullets becoming a pile of special cases, and it is what lets the descriptor grow (a location and an enemy kind with #708, an act and an agenda kind with #709) without any call site learning a new way to say "the source".

**One reachability predicate implements the bullets, and it is defined as an enumeration.** `engine::ability_source::reachable_sources` lists what an investigator can reach; `resolve` is a lookup *in that list*, not a second scan. The turn-menu enumerator lists from the first and the validator asks the second, so the menu and handler-acceptance cannot drift into disagreeing about which sources exist — the same by-construction property `check_activate_ability` already gave them for legality. `enumerate_fast_plays` consults it too, because the bullets are written once for all three ability icons and the fast window is the `[free]` half of the same sentence. This slice implements the control bullet; #708 and #709 add co-location and the board cards to the same function.

**Widening which sources are addressable does not widen what is legal.** Everything `Appendix_I_Initiation_Sequence.md` requires still runs, unchanged, after reachability has answered — *"Check play restrictions: determine if the card can be played, or if the ability can be initiated, at this time. (This includes verifying that the resolution of the effect has the potential to change the game state.)"*, and that the cost *"can be paid"*. An unpayable cost and a provably inert effect stay unoffered from every reachable source, which is what the integration test asserts source by source.

**Cost payment goes through the same predicate.** `require_source_reachable` re-resolves the source at the moment each cost is paid (#706's identity-addressing contract), so a cost cannot reach a card the activation was never allowed to name, and a source removed by an earlier cost is simply absent rather than a stale position pointing at a neighbour. `resolve_mut` decides *reachability* by calling `resolve` and only then re-finds the instance mutably — it never re-derives the bullets, which is the difference between one predicate and two that agree by luck until #708 makes a co-located threat area reachable by a non-controller.

## Considered options

**Widening the existing lookup zone by zone** — keep the bare `CardInstanceId` and have the validator search one more collection. This is the original framing of the problem, and Haunted 01098's ruling kills it (<https://arkhamdb.com/card/01098>), verbatim:

> Any investigator at the same location as the investigator with Haunted in their threat area may trigger the \[action\]\[action\] to discard Haunted, as per the FAQ \[V1.0, section 2.1\].

matching the printed bullet's *"all encounter cards in the threat area of **any** investigator at that location"*. The answer is therefore N investigators' collections selected by co-location, not one more collection on the acting investigator. It also cannot reach locations, acts or agendas at all, since none of them carries a card instance to name — so it fixes one bullet and leaves three, which is exactly how the current shape arose in the first place.

**Giving locations, acts and agendas a card-instance identity**, so the action's payload need not change at all. This dovetails with #290, and it is the option that keeps the wire still. It was rejected because it hands locations and enemies two identities apiece, and every call site that looks them up — which is most of the engine, by `LocationId` and `EnemyId` — then has to pick which one it means. `CandidateSource`'s own documentation already refused this once, for `CandidateSource::Location`: *"Locations have a `LocationId`, not a `CardInstanceId`, so this can't fold into `InPlay` (#553)."*

**One entity table with capability facets** — every board object in a single table, with abilities as one facet among many; the 2026-08-14 forward-compatibility register's radical option 4.4. Rejected because Chapter 1 *content* does not force it: 4.4's three motivating cases are Innsmouth and Hemlock Vale, years out, and the typed partition of the board survives this feature untouched. The descriptor is a vocabulary for *addressing* sources, which is what the rules bullets are about; it is not a claim that locations, enemies and cards are the same kind of thing.

## Consequences

**This is a wire break with no migration, and persisted games are discarded rather than migrated.** Sessions are reconstructed by deserializing a frozen seed state and replaying a `ResolveInput`-only action log over it, and there is no schema version anywhere in the protocol — so a seed whose continuation stack holds a parked `ActionResume::ActivateAbility { instance_id }` frame no longer deserializes. Nothing has shipped, so old games are deleted. **No version field is added here**: versioning is its own decision and belongs with #581.

**The server now fails loudly on a log it cannot replay.** Two gaps made a stale log look like something else. `GameSession::load` ignored each replayed action's outcome, so a logged action that the engine now *rejects* produced a session reconstructed from the prefix that still replayed — a silently wrong board rather than an error; it returns `SessionError::Replay { seq, reason }` instead. And `get_or_load_room` collapsed every load error into `None`, reporting an unreplayable game as a nonexistent one; it now logs at `error` and refuses to attach. This matters more than the payload change itself, because the log addresses the turn menu **by position**: an `OptionId` deserializes forever, and the action it selects is whatever the enumerator lists today.

**`AbilitySource` has one variant today, and that is deliberate.** A single-variant enum earns its keep by being the place the other three kinds land without touching a call site, and by making the question it answers — *which source*, not *which collection* — nameable in code. #708 and #709 are the tickets that fill it in.

**A cost that discards its source now routes by zone, because widening the source set made a panic reachable.** `discard_card_from_play` panics on an instance that is not in `cards_in_play`, and a `Cost::DiscardSelf` on a threat-area card would have reached it. A **panic reachable from player input must not ship**, so `discard_source` sends a card in play to its controller's discard pile and a threat-area card to the encounter discard (the existing `discard_from_threat_area`), and rejects cleanly for the one source that cannot be discarded at all — the investigator card. No corpus card prints such a cost; the rejection is deliberately unlifted with no tracking issue, as with `reject_incompatible_costs`.

**`Event::AbilityActivated` still carries a `CardInstanceId`, and the growth is a compile error rather than a panic.** Every source kind in scope names an instance, so the field is filled by destructuring — `let AbilitySource::InPlay(instance_id) = source;` in the handler, and an exhaustive `match` in `TurnAction::target`. Both stop compiling the day #709 adds a kind with no instance, which is when the event's field has to become optional and the option's anchor has to be decided. The alternative shapes — an `expect` on `source.instance()`, or a `map_or` fallback — would have made the same day a panic reachable from player input, or a menu entry silently anchored to nothing.
