# The engine names the surface a prompt renders on

The board-interactivity pass (#206) rests on one claim: **the client never decides where a control belongs — it reads the anchor the engine attached.** S0 (#535) established that for options by adding `OptionTarget` to `ChoiceOption`, and explicitly rejected the alternative of re-deriving targets from `label` strings. S6 (#541) is where that claim has to hold for everything left over, because there is no flat `.action-bar` behind it to catch what falls through.

Two things did fall through, and both were invisible until the bar was scheduled for deletion.

**The three genuinely-global open-turn actions were not global; they were unnamed.** `TurnAction::EndTurn`, `Resource` and `Draw` all derived `OptionTarget::Global`, so the wire carried three options a client could tell apart only by reading `"End turn"` / `"Gain resource"` / `"Draw"` — the string coupling S0 rejected, arriving through the back door. They are not un-anchored actions. They are actions anchored to surfaces the board did not yet render: a turn control, a resource pool, a player deck. So the anchor vocabulary grows to name them — `TurnControl(InvestigatorId)`, `ResourcePool(InvestigatorId)`, `PlayerDeck(InvestigatorId)`, and `EncounterDeck` for the scenario-wide one — and the client renders those surfaces rather than inferring them.

**A prompt with no options had nowhere to carry an anchor at all.** `InputKind::Confirm` prompts have an empty `options` list by construction, and there are two of them: the Mythos step-1.4 encounter draw (`dispatch/encounter.rs`) and the cosmetic skill-test `AcknowledgeOutcome` pause (#478). On the wire they are the same shape — same kind, same empty options, differing only in prose. The bar rendered both as an identical "Confirm" button, which is why the collision never surfaced. Homing one of them on an encounter-deck element requires telling them apart, so **`InputRequest` carries an anchor of its own**, and the anchor is a property of the *prompt*, not only of the options within it. The encounter draw is anchored to `EncounterDeck`; the acknowledge pause is un-anchored and renders on the skill-test result surface; the open-turn menu is anchored to `TurnControl`, which is also how the prompt strips, and the waiting strip other accounts see above the board, suppress its `"Choose an action"` text structurally instead of matching on the string.

**"Un-anchored" is spelled `None`, and `OptionTarget::Global` is gone.** `Global`'s own documentation read *"no board anchor"* — a null-object encoding of `Option`, and adding a second nullable field would have put three cases (`None`, `Some(Global)`, `Some(anchor)`) in front of every renderer where two must behave identically. Both `ChoiceOption::target` and `InputRequest::target` are `Option<OptionTarget>`. Removing the variant rather than deprecating it is what makes the migration compiler-verified: 40 sites across 15 files became build errors, so none was missed silently. Some sites read better for it — the Investigate anchor's `map_or(OptionTarget::Global, OptionTarget::Location)` is now the `map` it always meant to be.

**The cost of that shape, accepted knowingly: un-anchored is the silent default.** `ChoiceOption::new(id, label)` and `InputRequest::confirm(text)` produce un-anchored prompts, and `.at(target)` anchors them — one builder idiom for both types, chosen over a required positional argument so the 40 call sites read as prose. A future builder that forgets `.at(…)` therefore compiles, and its option lands on a prompt strip instead of on its card. That failure is caught by tests, not by the type: the engine tests that pin each anchor, and the end-to-end headless test that drives an open turn through the encounter draw to the skill-test acknowledgement without touching anything but the board. Those tests are load-bearing rather than incidental, and deleting one re-opens the hole. The builders whose options are always board entities narrow the cost further. The engine's candidate-list helper (hunter ties, spawn-engage ties, the attack-order pick, the slot make-room discard, the soak options) and `resolve_grounded_choice` each take a bare `OptionTarget` per option and nothing else, so an un-anchored candidate does not compile. `resolve_grounded_choice` is the one way to offer a choice among board entities from an effect: the evaluator's `Chosen` arms and the card-local natives (Dynamite Blast 01024, Crypt Chill 01167) all go through it. **An entity selection's labels are derived from its anchors**: a location or an enemy reads as its name, and a card instance (an investigator card included) as its card's name from the registry, falling back to the card code when the registry has no entry. A builder supplies no label, so it cannot forget one or name a different entity than it anchors.

**An investigator is named by their investigator card**, `CardInstance` of the card the panel already renders. Whom a hunter engages, a chosen investigator, and the soak prompt's "take it yourself" option all anchor there. A dedicated `Investigator(InvestigatorId)` variant was the alternative, and it was rejected: the client would map it straight back to the same card, so it would have been a second spelling of one surface, and every exhaustive `match` on anchors would carry it. The card is also where the rules put damage and horror, so for the soak prompt it is literally what is being chosen.

**A play from the discard pile anchors on the pile; its label names the permitting card.** Wendy's Amulet 01014 lets its controller play the topmost event of their discard pile, so that option anchors on `DiscardPile(InvestigatorId)`, the pile as a whole, the way `PlayerDeck` names a deck. The pile's top tile shows the event but not the Amulet, so the label names both: *"Play Emergency Cache (Wendy's Amulet)"*. A choice among the pile's cards (Scavenging 01073) anchors each card by its position instead, as the next paragraph says.

**A card outside play is anchored by its position, and the prompt by the area it is chosen from.** A searched card, a card looked at on top of a deck, and an Item in a discard pile have no board surface, and their labels are only their names. Old Book of Lore 01031's three cards and Scrying 01061's three were the same wire shape, three name buttons on the prompt strips, so the client could neither show the cards nor hide them from the players who must not see them (`glossary/Search.md`: *"that player is permitted to look at all of the cards in the searched area without revealing those cards to the other players"*). So each card is `DeckCard { investigator, index }`, `EncounterDeckCard { index }` or `DiscardCard { investigator, index }`, by position, which keeps two copies of one card apart the way `CommittedCard` does, and the client reads the code from its own state at that position. `InputRequest::target` names the area: `PlayerDeck` or `DiscardPile` for a whole deck or pile, and for the top N of a deck, `SearchedTopOfDeck`, `LookedAtTopOfDeck` or `LookedAtTopOfEncounterDeck`, its count cut to the cards the deck holds. **A search and a look are two areas, not one**, because they are otherwise identical on the wire, and FAQ 2.13 (`glossary/Search.md`) makes them different things: *"if an investigator "looks at" the top 3 cards of their deck, this is not a search effect."* The client takes the verb and what it shows other players from which one it is, never from the prompt's text. `resolve_card_choice` takes the anchors and the area, so a caller cannot offer such a card un-anchored. The **card modal** is the surface: the answering account sees the cards themselves, each offered one with its verb button, and it is their sole surface while it is up. The prompt stays a selection ([ADR 0015](0015-a-choice-printed-on-one-card-is-a-decision-not-a-selection.md)).

**A surface the engine names by role submits directly; a board entity opens a menu.** The pass's uniform rule is that even a single option opens a context menu, so the player always sees and confirms what they commit to. The four new surfaces are the deliberate exception — End turn, Gain resource, Draw and the encounter draw are buttons that submit on click. The rule exists because a location or an enemy is one entity carrying *several* possible actions, and the entity alone does not say which; a control whose label is `"End turn"` has no such ambiguity, and wrapping it in a one-item menu adds a click to defend against a confusion that cannot occur. The rule reaches **selections** — prompts offering board entities, which is what an anchor disambiguates. A prompt whose options are alternatives printed on one card is not one, and [ADR 0015](0015-a-choice-printed-on-one-card-is-a-decision-not-a-selection.md) exempts it on this paragraph's own rationale. The three per-investigator controls are always present on the active investigator's panel and disabled when no matching option is live, so the panel's shape does not change between phases — layout that jumps as prompts arrive is most of what made the bar worth retiring.

**The last thing that fell through was a choice *inside* an effect, and it fell through by having no source to name.** An `Effect::ChooseOne`'s branches are not board entities — they are alternatives printed on a card — so `step_choose_one` built its options with the un-anchored constructor and every one of them landed on the prompt strip. That was invisible while the only such card was What's Going On?! 01105, whose branches also rendered as their `Debug` form, so there was nothing on the strip worth putting anywhere else. Act 3, What Have You Done? 01110 is the second consumer and settles both halves at once: a branch carries the label it is offered under, and the option is anchored to the card the effect is *printed on*. Nothing in this ADR's principle had to change; what was missing was a value at the dispatch site. The evaluator's `EvalContext` carries the firing `AbilitySource` as its one source, the `From<AbilitySource> for OptionTarget` map turns it into the anchor, and `source_instance()` projects the in-play instance from it for the readers that want one — `AbilitySource::instance()` returns `None` for exactly the board sources (the act, the agenda, a location, an enemy). **Every dispatch site that holds a source sets it** — the activation path as well as the forced run and the reaction window — so First Aid 01019's damage-or-horror choice anchors to its own asset. A site that does not know its source leaves it unset; asserting which *kind* of source it holds would be a silent wrong answer rather than a build error.

That default is the same knowing cost as above, one layer down: an `Effect::ChooseOne` reached from a dispatch site with no source to name renders on a prompt strip and nothing complains. The threaded ones are pinned by tests naming their expected anchor, for the reason this ADR already gives — the type will not catch it.

## Consequences

The board is now the whole input surface, and there is no fallback: an option the engine anchors to a surface the client does not render is unreachable rather than merely misplaced. The prompt strips are the floor that keep this from being silent — each strip, in the dock on the tab of an investigator of the account's the request asks, or the lead's above the tabs, shows the prompt text except for the open-turn menu (an account the request doesn't ask sees the text on the waiting strip above the board instead), and remains the home for un-anchored options and for Confirm/Pass. It is also, for now, the home for options anchored to `OptionTarget::CommittedCard`, the ST.7 result-order pick's committed cards. No board surface draws the committed list yet, so the strips carry them under the engine's label, which names the card, rather than leave them unreachable. That is a deliberate stopgap with a terminal condition: when #1322 builds a committed-card surface, the strips' `CommittedCard` arm goes. No option anchored to a card outside play (`DeckCard`, `EncounterDeckCard` or `DiscardCard`) reaches the strips: the card modal is their surface, and it shows a search's cards, Scrying's looked-at cards and a discard pile's. While the card modal is up, the strips carry none of its cards. Adding a prompt to the engine now carries a client obligation: either anchor it to a surface that exists, or leave it un-anchored and accept the strips.
