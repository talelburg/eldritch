# Initiation is one gate; timing is not part of it

"May this card be played, or this ability be initiated, right now?" was answered in six places: the in-play reaction scan, the act/agenda reaction scan, the hand Fast-event scan, the forced-trigger collector, the activation validator and the play validator. Each assembled its own set of checks, and the sets disagreed. The play-ban was asked by the play validator and not by the hand Fast-event scan, so with Dissonant Voices 01165 (*"You cannot play assets or events."*) in his threat area Roland was still offered Dodge 01023 in a reaction window (#917). That was the same ban #852 had already fixed on one path, forgotten on the next. Usage limits were counted on reactions and never on activated abilities, and eligibility tags were read on every path but activation.

Appendix I puts the question in one place for every path (`data/rules-reference/rules/Appendix_I_Initiation_Sequence.md`):

> When a player wishes to initiate a triggered ability or play a card, that player first declares his or her intent. There are two preliminary confirmations that must be made before the process of initiating an ability or playing a card may begin. These are:
>
> - Check play restrictions: determine if the card can be played, or if the ability can be initiated, at this time. (This includes verifying that the resolution of the effect has the potential to change the game state.) If the play restrictions are not met, abort this process.
> - Determine the cost (or costs, if multiple costs are required) to play the card or initiate the ability. If it is established that the cost (taking modifiers into account) can be paid, proceed with the remaining steps of this sequence.

**So there is one initiation gate, `engine::dispatch::initiation::check`, and every path asks it.** It owns exactly these checks: the controller is Active; the ability resolves at its address through the side-in-effect / grant funnel; its eligibility tag; the potential to change the game state; its usage limit; the play-ban; and whether the cost can be paid. A path passes the candidate (controller, source, code, ability address) and an `InitiationKind`, and the gate resolves the ability itself, so the offer and the pick are judged by the same code. Recording a use (Appendix I step 3, so a cancelled use still counts) lives beside it as `record_initiation`.

**Timing is not part of the gate.** *When* something may be initiated is each path's own question, and the paths genuinely differ on it: a reaction scan has a timing cell, `trigger_matches` and its co-location and self-binding scoping; the play validator has the turn/Fast/"play only during your turn" matrix; activation has its action points. Slots, the hand index and designator targets stay with their paths too. The action cost, the Frozen in Fear surcharge and attacks of opportunity sit with neither: they are **taking an action**, one step in `engine::dispatch::actions::take` (item 04 of the 2026-09 architecture review, #921; card `id="c4"` in the [report](https://talelburg.github.io/eldritch/architecture-review-2026-09.html)). Every action is taken through it: the basic actions, a non-fast play and an action-cost ability. A fast play or fast ability spends no action, so it takes none. Taking an action re-asks Status as part of "may take an action". That is the only Status check a basic action gets, since basic actions never reach this gate, and it is redundant for plays and abilities; the step asks it of every kind alike rather than taking a per-kind shape. The step must not fold timing and legality back together either. Legality is what has to be identical across paths. Timing is what differs. Put both in one predicate, and either that predicate grows a branch per path or each path goes back to carrying its own copy of the legality checks, which is the six-place drift this replaces.

**Forced abilities go through the gate too**, as `InitiationKind::Forced`. The kind gets the change-state and eligibility checks: `glossary/Ability.md`, *"If a forced ability does not have the potential to change the game state, the ability does not initiate."* It gets the usage-limit check too, because a limit counts initiations and a forced ability initiates: `glossary/Limits_and_Maximums.md`, *"Each instance of an ability with such a limit may be initiated X times during the designated period."* It skips cost, because a forced ability is not paid for. Status stays with the trigger scan's board walk (ADR 0018), because the elimination game end deliberately reaches the investigator being eliminated. Keeping forced abilities outside the gate would leave a seventh copy of the eligibility check, the class of bug #791 fixed.

**A Fast event offered from hand is `InitiationKind::Play`, not `Reaction`.** It is played (`glossary/Fast.md`: *"A fast card does not cost an action to be played and is not played using the "Play" action."*), and a card in hand is not an ability source. Asking as `Play` gives it the play-ban by construction. That is what makes #917 impossible rather than fixed.

**A refusal is a typed `Refusal`, kept local to the gate.** One variant per check, so the reaction window's lapse reason reads why an option lapsed instead of re-deriving it. A withdrawn option the gate still passes has fallen out of its scan's scoping, which the gate does not own, and is labelled `OutOfScope`. It renders to the existing `Cow<'static, str>` where it meets `EngineOutcome::Rejected`. Engine-wide typed rejections were out of scope, and handlers keep returning strings at their boundary, so `EngineOutcome` does not change. The event stream does: `Event::ReactionOptionLapsed` reaches the client, and its `LapseReason` gains `UsageLimitReached`, `NotActive`, `PlayBanned` and `OutOfScope`, while `NoLongerEligible` narrows from the unexplained residual to a false eligibility condition.

*Folded #965 (the forced status filter moved from the per-point collector arms into the one trigger scan's board walk, [ADR 0018](0018-one-trigger-scan-walks-the-whole-board.md)), #967 (forced abilities get the usage-limit check), #994 (cost, surcharge and attacks of opportunity move into the take-an-action step), #995 (plays and abilities are taken through it too).*
