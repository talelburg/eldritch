# An effect resolves in full before its consequences

Abandoned and Alone 01015 prints *"**Revelation** - Take 2 direct horror and remove all cards in your discard pile from the game."* With Psychosis 01099 in the threat area (*"**Forced** - After you take 1 or more horror: Take 1 direct damage."*), the engine dealt Psychosis's damage before the discard pile left the game. A multi-aspect effect parks the aspects still to come and pushes the current one above them, so a timing coordinator that aspect raised sat above the rest of the effect, and the last-in, first-out drive loop walked its cells first. The rules say the opposite. `glossary/Effects.md`: *"All aspects of an effect have timing priority over all "after..." triggering conditions that might arise as a consequence of that effect."* `glossary/Then.md`: *"The post-then aspect of an effect has timing priority over all other indirect consequences of the resolution of the pre-then aspect."*

**So a condition raised inside an effect waits for the effect.** Its `when` cell and its resolution still happen inline, where the effect raised it. Once its cursor reaches `At`, its coordinator leaves the stack and **parks** on the effect, on the `EffectBase` marker pushed beneath every effect's root `EffectFrame`. When the effect has resolved in full, the marker is exposed, pops, and puts its parked coordinators back on the stack. Each walks its `at` cell and then its `after` cell as before. `coordinator::park_in_effect_base` and `evaluator::close_effect_base` hold the mechanism. This does not contradict [ADR 0008](0008-a-triggering-condition-resolves-inside-its-own-sequence.md)'s quote of `glossary/After.md`, *"the moment immediately after the specified timing point or triggering condition has fully resolved"*: that clause places the `after` cell after the condition, and `Effects.md`'s timing-priority clause governs what comes between a condition and its `after` cell, the rest of the effect that raised it.

One mechanism covers every multi-aspect shape. A `Seq`, a `ForEach`, the branches of an `If` or a `ChooseOne`, and a native's tail ([ADR 0020](0020-a-native-resolves-in-program-order.md)) all sit above the same marker, so a new shape gets the rule without code of its own. Cunning Distraction 01078 evades every enemy at the location before Pickpocketing 01046 is offered, and Dynamite Blast 01024 lands every deal before Hypochondria 01100's direct horror.

## Which effect

**The outermost effect still resolving within the current nested sequence.** The coordinator parks on the deepest `EffectBase` above the nearest boundary. Every other frame between is transparent, including a coordinator still resolving its own condition: `DamagePlaced`, raised while `DamageDealt` resolves, parks where `DamageDealt` parks. The deepest base is the target because an effect can nest another without starting a new sequence. Drawn to the Flame 01064's draw resolves the drawn card's Revelation as an effect of its own, and `Then.md` has the play's *"Then, discover 2 clues at your location."* land before that Revelation's consequences.

A consequence never waits past a boundary. There are four:

- **A timing cell.** An ability initiated from a cell starts a nested sequence. `glossary/Nested_Sequences.md`: *"Within this sequence, if the use of a \[reaction\] or **Forced** ability leads to a new triggering condition, the game pauses and starts a new sequence"*, which *"must complete before returning to the sequence that spawned it."* Its Guard Dog example resolves Agnes Baker's reaction to the Goat Spawn's horror *"Before resolving the Goat Spawn's defeat"*. So a reaction's own consequences open when the reaction's effect ends, not when the effect that raised its window ends.
- **A skill test.** The Official FAQ on Shrivelling 01060 (`data/official-faq/Frequently_Asked_Questions.md`): *"You would take 1 horror immediately as you reveal the symbol, during “ST.3 Reveal chaos token” … If you had any reactions to taking that horror (such as Agnes Baker’s ability), you would trigger it then, before resolving the rest of the attack."* Roland Banks 01001's after-defeat reaction is still offered during the fight that defeated the enemy.
- **An act or agenda advance.** `glossary/Act_Deck_and_Agenda_Deck.md`, step 2: *"Flip the advancing card over and follow the instructions on the reverse ("b") side."* The reverse is a step of the advance, not an "after…" consequence of it. Without this boundary, an act advanced inside an effect (act 01110's objective, fired from the `at` cell of the Ghoul Priest's defeat) would park its `ActAdvanced` past the advance's own frame and finalize before its reverse ran. The terminal-act assertion in `advance_reverse.rs` refuses that, and every default-deck playthrough of The Gathering reaches it.
- **An elimination.** Step 0 fires game-end abilities on weaknesses that steps 1–6 then remove ([ADR 0008](0008-a-triggering-condition-resolves-inside-its-own-sequence.md) runs those steps after the whole sequence), so the abilities run before those steps. No corpus card reaches it: Cover Up 01007's game-end ability is a `when`-cell one.

**The skill-test boundary is an interpretation where the sources pull different ways.** Drawn to the Flame drawing Rotting Remains 01163 (*"**Revelation** - Test \[willpower\] (3). For each point you fail by, take 1 horror."*) deals the horror inside a test. `Then.md` read alone would hold Psychosis's damage until after the 2 clues are discovered. The FAQ puts a reaction to horror taken during a test inside the test, and where it contradicts the Rules Reference, *"the text of this document takes precedence"* (`data/official-faq/Rulings_and_Clarifications.md`). So Psychosis fires inside Rotting Remains' test, and the discovery comes after it.

Other frames between a coordinator and its base are aspects of the effect and do not bound it. Inside Drawn to the Flame, an encounter card's disposal precedes the `at` and `after` cells of `EncounterCardDrawn`. A move's arrival precedes those of `LeftLocation`. No corpus card listens there.

## `at` and `if` wait with `after`

**This is an interpretation, not a citation.** `Effects.md` names only *"after..."* triggering conditions. ADR 0008 already places `at` and `if` after the condition's impact, and parking at `At` keeps a condition's last two cells together, with one split point. Act 01110 What Have You Done? is the motivating case: *"**Objective** - If the Ghoul Priest is Defeated, advance."* Its reverse ends the scenario, so an effect that defeats the Ghoul Priest finishes before the objective fires.

## Release order

**The windows open in the order each condition reached its `at` cell**, the first parked on top. A coordinator parks when it reaches `At` on top of the stack, not when its condition resolved, and those differ in two places no test pins. Dynamite Blast's lethal enemy damage queues caller-owned `EnemyDefeated` frames beneath the native's tail, so they reach `At` after both investigators' deals, and Roland's window opens after Hypochondria's. Several caller-owned coordinators queued by one call reach `At` in reverse, as their windows opened before. Parking each as it was queued would move ADR 0008's caller-owned `when`-cell reject to release time.

FIFO is what Agnes Baker 01004 needs. Abandoned and Alone's horror raises `DamageDealt`, and `DamagePlaced` nested inside it reaches `At` first, so her *"After 1 or more horror is placed on Agnes Baker"* reaction is offered before Psychosis's *"After you take 1 or more horror"* damage, and both after the discard pile is gone.

## What stays as it was

A parked coordinator is off the stack, inside its base's payload, so nothing that reads the stack sees it. Its own `TimingPoint` frames are pushed only after release, directly above it, so `advance_or_finish_emit` still finds it on top. `Effect::Cancel` targets the topmost coordinator still at `When`, and a parked one never is. The marker is Driven and completes under a latched resolution ([ADR 0004](0004-a-latched-resolution-cancels-opportunities-not-resolutions.md)), and the coordinators it releases meet the drive loop's gate one by one as they reach the top, so a held reaction window is cancelled like any other opportunity. A released coordinator never parks again: the base that released it was the deepest above its boundary.
